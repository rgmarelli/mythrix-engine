1. Rabbi Nechunia ben Hakana said, "One verse states, 'And now men see not the light which is bright in the skies,' (Job 37:21). Another verse states, 'He made darkness His hiding-place' (Psalms 18:12). And it says, 'Clouds and darkness surround Him' (Psalms 97:2). This is a difficulty. A third scripture reconciles them: 'Even the darkness is not too dark for You, But the night shines as the day; the darkness is as the light' (Psalms 139:12)."

2. Rabbi Berechiah said, "It is written, 'Now the earth was tohu and bohu' (Genesis 1:2). What does 'was' mean? That the tohu already was. What is tohu? Something that confounds [m'taha] people. And what is bohu? Tohu was, and it returned to bohu. And what is bohu? A thing that has substance in it, as is written, 'It is inside it [bo hu].'"

3. [Rabbi Berechiah continued,] "And why does the Torah begin with [the letter] Bet? To start with a blessing [b'racha]. From this, we learn that the Torah is called a blessing, as it is said, 'Full [with the] blessing of YHVH, possess the sea and the south' (Deuteronomy 33:23). There is no sea other than Torah, as it is said, 'And broader than the sea' (Job 11:9). And what is 'Full with the blessing of God'? It means that every place that [the letter] Bet is said is the language of blessing. As we say, 'b'reishit'. And there is no beginning [ראשית] except wisdom, as it is said, 'The beginning [ראשית] of wisdom is the fear of YHVH' (Psalms 111:10). And there is no wisdom except [as the result of a] blessing, as it is said, 'And Elohim blessed Solomon,' and it is written, 'YHVH gave wisdom to Solomon' (1 Kings 5:26). This is comparable to a king that marries his daughter to his son and says to him [the son] at the wedding, 'Do with her as you will.'"

4. [Rabbi Berechiah continued,] "And what is the proof that this 'b'rachah' is from the word 'blessing' [baruch]? Perhaps it is from the language of [the word] 'knee' [berech]. As it is written, 'For to Me every knee will bend' (Isaiah 45:23). [God is] the Place [towards which] all knees bend. To what parable is this similar? To people who seek to see the face of the king, but do not know where His house is. First, they ask, 'Where is the house of the king?', and afterward [they can ask], 'Where is the king?' Therefore [it is written], 'For to Me every knee will bend' — even the highest ones — 'Every tongue will swear [loyalty to God]' (Isaiah 45:23)."

5. Rabbi Rachumai sat and expounded, "Why is it written 'And filled with the blessing of YHVH, inherit the sea and the south' (Deuteronomy 33:23)? In every place [where there is the letter] Bet, it is blessed. 'It' is the filling, as it is said, 'And the filling is the blessing of YHVH.' From there, the needy drink, and from the filling [God] took counsel in the beginning. To what parable is this similar? To a king who wants to build his palace in strong cliffs. He cut stones and chiseled boulders, and a spring of great waters — living waters — went out from it. The king said, 'Since I have waters bursting forth, I will plant a garden and I will delight in it — I and the whole world.' This is as it is written, 'And I was with Him as a confidant, and I was a source of delight every day [יום יום], to rejoice before Him at all times' (Proverbs 8:30). The Torah said, 'For two thousand years I was in the bosom of the Blessed Holy One,' as it is said 'A day, a day [יום יום]'. A day of the Blessed Holy One is a thousand years, as it is said, 'For a thousand years in Your eyes is like a yesterday that has passed' (Psalms 90:4). From then on, it is for times — as it is said, 'At every time' — but the remainder is for the world — as it is said, 'And I will breathe my praise to you' (Isaiah 48:9). What does it mean 'my praise'? As it is written, 'A song of praise of David, I will raise You high' (Psalms 145:1) Why is it 'praise'? Because 'I will raise You high.' And what is this elevation? Because 'I will bless Your name forever' (Psalms 145:2)."

6. [Rabbi Rachumai continued,] "And what is a 'blessing'? To what parable is this comparable? To a king that plants trees in his garden, and even though rain always comes down and waters [them], and the land is damp and constantly waters them, still, he needs to water the trees from the spring. As it is said, 'The beginning of wisdom is the fear of YHVH, good intelligence to all who do them' (Psalms 111:10). And lest you say that it lacks something, it continues, 'His praise stands forever' (Psalms 111:10)."

7. Rabbi Amorai sat and expounded, "Why is it written 'And full of the blessing of YHVH, he will take possession of the west and south' (Deuteronomy 33:23)? Thus Moses said, 'If you walk in His laws, you will inherit this world and the next world.' The world to come is compared to the sea, as it is said 'And [it is] wider than the sea' (Job 11:9). And this world is compared to the south, as it is said '[Give me a blessing,] for you have placed me in the land of the Negev [the southern desert]' (Joshua 15:19), and from our targum [this is translated into Aramaic as], 'Behold, the earth is the south' (Targum Jonathan, Joshua 15:19)."

8. [Rabbi Amorai continued,] "And further, why did the Blessed Holy One add the [letter] hey in Abraham [when God changed Abram’s name to Abraham] rather than any other letter? In order that all the organs of man (Adam) will merit life in the world to come, which is compared to the sea [Ed.: Hebrew manuscripts often read "day," but it likely should be "sea." See above, #7]. So to speak, 'With him [Abraham] the building [i.e. - the structure of man] was completed.' As it is written, 'For in the image of Elohim, he made the Adam' (Genesis 9:6). Abraham in gematria is 248, which is the number of the organs of an adam."

9. [Rabbi Amorai continued,] "Why is it written 'He will inherit (ירשה)' (Deuteronomy 33:23)? It would have sufficed to say 'inherit' (רש) [in the imperative form]. Rather, even the Blessed Holy One is included, and thus, it is, 'inherit Yah [ירשה = רש יה]. To what is this comparable? To a king who had two treasuries and set aside one of them. After many days [literally, 'at the end days'], he said to his son, 'Take what there is in these two treasuries.' The son said, 'Perhaps he did not give me what he set aside.' He [the king] said, ‘Take everything.' And so it was written 'West and south, inherit Yah' (Deuteronomy 33:23) Inherit Yah and He will give you everything — if only you would guard My way."

10. Rabbi Bun said, "Why is it written 'I was set up from eternity [מעולם], from the beginning [מראש], before the earth' (Proverbs 8:23)? What is 'from eternity [מעולם]'? That it must be concealed [להעלימו] from the whole world. As it is written, '[God] also puts eternity [העולם] in their hearts [i.e. - minds]' (Ecclesiastes 3:11) — Don’t read 'eternity [העולם], rather 'hidden [העלם]. The Torah said, 'I was first, [in order] to be head [ראש] of the world,' as it is said 'I was set up from eternity, from a head [מראש].' And if you would say, 'Perhaps the earth preceded her,' the Torah says 'From before the earth' (Proverbs 8:23), as it is said, 'In the beginning [בראשית], Elohim created the Heaven and the Earth' (Genesis 1:1). And why does it say 'created'? [With the 'head' were] the needs for everything created, and afterward Elohim. And what is written after it? 'The Heavens and the Earth.' (Genesis 1:1)."

11. [Rabbi Bun continued,] And what is ['Elohim also created one opposite the other' (Ecclesiastes 7:14)? [God] created bohu and put its place in peace and created tohu and put its place in evil. Bohu is in peace as it is said 'The One Who creates peace in His heights' (Job 25:2). This teaches that Michael, the right-hand prince of the Blessed Holy One, is water and hail, and Gavriel, the left-hand prince of the Blessed Holy One, is fire. And the Prince of Peace decides between them. Thus it was written, 'The One Who creates peace in His heights.' (Job 25:2)"

12. [Rabbi Bun continued,] "And from where do we learn that tohu is in evil? As it is written, 'The one makes peace and creates evil' (Isaiah 45:7). How is this? Evil is from tohu and peace is from bohu. [God] created tohu and put its place in evil, as it is said, 'The one who makes peace and creates evil.' [God] created bohu and placed it in peace, as it is said, The one who creates peace in His heights' (Job 25:2)."

13. And Rabbi Bun sat and expounded more, "Why is it written, '[God] forms light and creates darkness' (Isaiah 45:7)? [Since] there is substance in light, it is written, as ‘formation.’ [Since] there is not substance in darkness, it is written as ‘creation.’ As you say, '[God] forms mountains and creates wind' (Amos 4:13). And if you want, say, that light has existence in it, as it is written, 'And Elohim said, ‘Let there be light,’ and there is no existence except by making, [therefore] call it 'formation.' Darkness, since it does not involve making, only separation and setting aside, it is called ‘creation’ [בריאה], as it is said, 'So-and-so became healthy [הבריא].'"

14. [Rabbi Bun continued,] "Why is [the letter] Bet closed on all sides and open in the front? To teach you that it is the house [bayit] of the world and that the Blessed Holy One, is the place of the world yet the world is not His place. Don’t read 'Bet', rather 'bayit'. This what is written, 'With wisdom [בחכמה], a house is built, and with understanding [בתבונה], it is established' (Proverbs 24:3).”

15. [Rabbi Bun continued,] "And to what is [the letter] Bet similar? To a person that it is formed with wisdom: closed on all sides but open in its front. [But] the [letter] Aleph is open from its back. This is to say that the tail of the Bet is open from behind, and if it were not thus, humans would not be sustained. So too, if it were not for the Bet in the tail of the Aleph, the world would not be sustained."

16. Rabbi Rechumai said, "The light preceded the world since 'Cloud and fog surround Him' (Psalm 97:2), as it is said, 'And God said, ‘There will be light,’ and there was light' (Genesis 1:3). They said to Him, ‘Before the formation of Israel, your son, will You make him a crown?’ He responded ‘yes.’ To what parable is this comparable? To a king that yearned for a son and found a beautiful, richly-adorned, and exalted crown and rejoiced greatly. He said, 'This is for my son’s head, as it is beautiful for him!' They said to him, 'And you know that your son is worthy?' He replied 'Silence! Thusly, this arose in my mind, and it is known!' As it is said, 'And He thinks thoughts' (Samuel 2 14:14)."

17. Rabbi Amorai sat and expounded, “Why is [the letter] Aleph at the beginning [of the aleph-bet]? That she [the letter Aleph] was before everything, even the Torah."

18. [Rabbi Amorai continued,] “And why is [the letter] Bet close to her [the letter] Aleph]? Because she [Bet] was the beginning. And why does she have a tail? To show which place she was from. There are those that say from there the world is sustained."

19. [Rabbi Amorai continued,] “And why is [the letter] Gimel third? Because it has three parts, and to inform us that she bestows [גומלת] acts of lovingkindness." 
[They asked him,] "And didn't Rabbi Akiva [already] say, ‘Why is Gimel in three parts? Because she weans [גומלת], grows [מתגדלת], and sustains [מתקיימת],’ as it is said ‘And the boy grew up [ויגדל] and was weaned [ויגמל]' (Genesis 21:8)." 
He said to them, "It is my words [that Rabbi Akiva said], for he grew up [גודל] and bestowed [גומל] lovingkindness to his neighbors, his people, and those who trusted him."

20. [They asked,] “And why is there a tail at the bottom of Gimel?" 
[Rabbi Amorai] said, "The Gimel has a head above and is similar to a pipe. What is a pipe? Something that draws [water] from above and empties below. So too, Gimel draws by way of the head and empties by way of the tail. This is Gimel.”

21. [Note: there are different textual variations of this text. Here is the version on Sefaria.] 
Rabbi Yochanon said, “On the second day, the angels were created, as it is written, 'He rafters the beams [of His upper chambers] in the waters' (Psalms 104:3), and it is written 'He makes the winds His messengers, fiery flames His servants' (Psalms 104:4)." 
But Rabbi Levitas ben Tavros said, "Everyone, even Rabbi Yochanan, agrees that the water already existed. But it was on the second day that ''He rafters the beams [of His upper chambers] in the waters,' the one who 'makes the clouds his chariot,' and the one who 'walks on the wings of the wind.' But His messengers were not created until the fifth day."

[The Vilna Gaon's commentary suggests the following version.]
Rabbi Yochanan said, "The angels were created on the second day. It is therefore written, 'He rafters the beams [of His upper chambers] in the waters, making the clouds a chariot, moving on the wings of the wind" (Psalm 104:3). It is then written, 'He makes the wind His angels' (Psalms 104:4)." 
Rabbi Chaninah said, "The creation of the angels was on the fifth day, as is said, 'And the birds that flew over the earth' (Genesis 1:20)." 
Rabbi Lulyani ben Tavrai said, "All agree that they [the angels] were not created on day one."

22. [Rabbi Levitas ben Tavrus continued,] "And all agree that [the angels] were not created on the first day, lest people might say, 'Michael drew out the south of Heaven, Gabriel [drew out] the north, and the Blessed Holy One is the one who made order in the middle. Rather, I am YHVH, who makes everything, Who stretches out the heavens by myself, who spreads out the land, unaided [מאתי]' (Isaiah 44:24). It is written [as] ‘who is with me’ [מי אתי]. [To say,] 'I am He that planted this tree so that all of the world would delight in it. I laid everything in it, and I called its name 'all,' because everything depends upon it, everything comes out from it, everything needs it, to it everything looks, for it they wait, and, from there, souls fly in joy. I was by myself when I did this. An angel will not be aggrandized on it to say, 'I was before you,' because at the time that I laid out the earth, and planted and rooted this tree in her, I rejoiced together, and I rejoiced in them. Who was with Me [מי אתי] that I revealed to him my secret?'"

23. Rabbi Rachumi said, “From your words, it could be learned that the Blessed Holy One created the needs of this world before the heavens.” 
He [Rabbi Levitas ben Tavrus] replied, “Yes. ָA parable, to what it is similar: to a king who seeks to plant a tree in his garden. He searches throughout the whole garden to know if there is a spring of flowing water to place the tree upon, and he does not find one. He says, ‘I will dig for water, and I will bring forth a spring so that the tree can be sustained.’ He dug, and out came a spring flowing with living waters. Afterward, he planted the tree, and it stood and produced fruit. It was successful in its roots because it always drinks from the spring."

24. Rabbi Yanai said, “The Earth was created before the Heavens, as it is said, ‘[In the day of YHVH God’s making of the] Earth and the Heavens' (Genesis 2:4).’”
They said to him, “Isn’t it written ‘[In the beginning, God created] the Heavens and the Earth’ (Genesis 1:1)?” 
He said to them, "To what does this compare? To a king who acquires a beautiful object, but it was not complete, so he did not give it a name. He said, ‘I will complete it and prepare its pedestal and accessories, and then I will give it a name.’ Thus it is written, ‘From eternity You established the Earth’ (Psalms 102:6), and afterward ‘And Heaven is the work of Your hands’ (Psalms 102:26). And it says, ‘He covered Himself with a garment of light, spread out the Heavens like a curtain, and set the waters as the rafters of His chambers’ (Psalms 104:2-3). Futher, it says, ‘He makes the winds His messengers and fiery flames His servants’ (Psalms 104:4), and afterward ‘He established the Earth on its foundation so that it will never [עולם ועד] be moved’ (Psalms 104:5). When He completed the pedestal, [the Earth] was strengthened by it, as is said, 'It will never be moved.’ What is its name? ‘Va’ed' [ועד] and the pedestal is ‘Earth' [עולם], thus it is ‘Olam Va’ed' [עולם ועד].”

25. Rabbi Berachyah said, “Why is it written, ‘And Elohim said, ‘Let there be light,’ and there was [ויהי] light’ (Genesis 1:3), and not, ‘And there was [והיה] light’? A parable: a king that had a nice object and set it aside until he prepared for it a place and put it there. As it is written, 'Let there be light, and there was light' — that it already was [היה]."

26. Rabbi Amorai said, “Why is it written, ‘YHVH is a man of war’ (Exodus 15:3)?" Mar Rachumai said to him, "My teacher, you should not ask a simple word! Hear me, and I will advise you." He [Mar Rachumai] said to him, "To what may this be compared? To a king who had beautiful dwellings, who gave a name to every single one of them, and each one of them was better than the last. He said, 'I will give this dwelling that is named Aleph to my son. But the one named Yod is also good, and the one named Shin is also good!' What did he do? He gathered together all three, made one name from them, and made one house from them." He [Rabbi Amorai] said to him, "Until when will you conceal your words?!" He [Mar Rachumai] said to him, "My son, Aleph is first, Yod is second to her, Shin includes the whole world. Why does Shin include the whole world? Because teshuvah [repentance] is written with her."

27. His [Mar Rachumai's] students asked him, “What is Dalet?” He said to them, "To what can this be compared? To ten kings that were in the same place. Though all of them were rich, one of them was not as wealthy as the others. Even though he was very wealthy, he is called poor [dal] in relation to the wealthy ones.

28. They said to him [Mar Rachumai], "What is He? He was angry and said to them, "Did I not say to you not to ask me about a later thing and afterward about the previous?" They said to him, "But He is written after [Dalet]!" He said to them, "It is fitting for it to have been written, ' Gimel He,' so why is it written, ' Gimel Dalet'?" He said to them, "The Gimel was switched in place of Dalet, in the head in the place of He. Dalet in the embellishment of the place of He."

29. [Mar Rachumai's students asked him,] "What is He?" He said, "Lower He and upper He."

30. [Mar Rachumai's students] said to him, "What is Vav?" 
He said to them, "With six borders, the world is sealed." 
They said to him, "But isn't Vav one?" 
He said to them, "Is it not written, 'He spreads light like a garment' (Psalms 104:2)?"

31. Rabbi Amorai said, "Where is the Garden of Eden?" He [Mar Rachumai] said to him, "On the Earth."

32. [Mar Rachumai continued, citing an earlier teaching.] "Rabbi Ishmael expounded to Rabbi Akiva, 'What is it written, '[Et] the heavens and [et] the earth' (Genesis 1:1)? If it did not say this, people would say that the heavens and earth were gods.' He [Akiva] said to him, 'By the service of the divine! You touched [the verse], but you did not ascertain — and yet you spoke! [The word] et [serves to] include the sun, moon, stars, and constellations; v'et includes trees, grasses, and the Garden of Eden."

33. They [Mar Rachumai's students] said to him [Mar Rachumai], "Isn't it written, 'He has cast down from the heavens [to the] land, the majesty [tiferet] of Israel' (Lamentations 2:1), and, afterward, they fell." He said to them, "If you read it, you did not repeat it, and if you repeated it, you did not do it a third time! A parable, to what may this be compared? To a king that had a beautiful crown on his head and a beautiful cloak on his shoulders. An evil tiding came to him, and he cast the crown from his head and the cloak [note a mistake in the Sefaria Hebrew] from before him.

34. They [Mar Rachumai's students] asked him, "Why is Chet formed openly and vowelized with a small patach?" He said to them, "Because all the directions [ruchot] are closed except for the north, which is open for good and evil." They said to him, "For good?!? But isn't it written, 'And behold a stormy wind [ruach] came from the north, a great cloud and flashing fire' (Ezekiel 1:4). And there is not fire except furious anger, as is written, And fire went out from before YHVH and consumed them, and they died' (Leviticus 10:2)." He said to them, "This is not difficult. Here it is at a time when Israel does the will of The Place, while there is at a time when Israel does not do His will. At the time that Israel does not do His will, fire comes close, and at the time that they do His will, the trait of compassion rolls and surrounds, as it is written, 'Lifting up iniquity and passing over transgression' (Micah 7:18)."

35. [Mar Rachumai continued,] "A parable, to what can this be compared? To a king who wants to punish his servants and whip them. A leader stands and asks why. He says, 'Because of this.' He [the leader] says, 'Your servants never did this thing. I will be their guarantor, and you will investigate them, whether this or that.' The king's anger subsided.

36. His [Mar Rachumai's] students asked and said, "Why is Dalet thick on the side?" He said to them, "Because the segol is in the small patach, as is said, 'Openings of the world' (Psalms 24:7). He placed there a patach above and a segol below, and it becomes thick."

37. [Mar Rachumai's students asked,] "What is patach?" [He said,] "An opening [petach]." [They asked,] "And what is an opening?" [He replied,] "It is a north wind, which opens to the whole world. From the gate through which the evil came out, the good comes out." [They asked,] "And what is 'good'?” He mocked them, "Didn't I say to you, 'a small patach'?" They said to him, "We forgot. Teach us." He repeated [the teachings from 34 and 36, above]. He said to them, "To what can this be compared? To a king who has a throne. Sometimes he takes it on his arm and sometimes on his head." They said to him, "Why?" [He replied,] "Because it is beautiful and [it would be] a pity to sit upon it." They said, "Where does he place it on his head?" He said to them, "In the open mem, as it is written, 'Truth sprouts from the land, and righteousness looks down from the heavens.' (Psalms 85:12)"

38. Rabbi Amorai sat and expounded, "Why is it written, 'YHVH loves the gates of Zion more than all the [other] dwellings of Jacob' (Psalms 87:2)? 'The gates of Zion' - these are the openings of the world. There is no gate that is not an opening, as we say, 'Open for us the gates of compassion' (High Holiday Prayers). Thus, the Blessed Holy One said, 'I love the gates of Zion, when they are open.' Why? Because they are from the side of evil. But when Israel are good before The Place and fitting to be opened to good, the Blessed Holy One loves them ' more than the dwellings of Jacob.' All of them are peace - as is written, 'And Jacob was a simple man, sitting in tents' (Genesis 25:27)."

39. [Rabbi Amorai continued,] "This is like two men: one is invited to do evil and did good, and one is invited to do good and did good. Who do they praise more? The one who is accustomed to do evil and did good; perhaps he will do it a second time, as is written, 'YHVH loves the gates of Zion more than the dwellings of Jacob' (Psalms 87:2), all of which are peace, as is written, 'And Jacob was a simple man, sitting in tents' (Genesis 25:27)."

40. His students asked him, “What is cholem?” He [Rabbi Amorai] said to them, “A soul [neshamah]. Its name is cholem because if you listen, your body will recover for the future of its builders, but if you rebel against it, your illnesses will return upon your head — so cholim are on her head."

41. And further, they said, "Every dream is in a cholem, and every white pearl is in a cholem, as is written, 'achlamah [ed. - one of the stones on the choshen, usually translated as chrystal or amethyst]' (Exodus 28:19)."

42. He said to them, "Enter and hear the details of the vowel points of the Torah of Moses." He sat and expounded to them, "Chirik hates [son'e] the wicked and chastises them, and on its side are jealousy, hatred, and contention. As it is written, "And gnash [chorek] their teeth [shinav] at them" (Psalms 37:12). Don't read chorek, rather rochek [distance]. Distance these traits from yourself and distance the evil from yourself. All the more so, goodness will cleave to you."

43. [He continued,] "Chirik — do not say chirik, rather 'kerach [bald or ice],' since every place where a chirik is attached remains bald [or ice], as is written, 'And cleared'(Exodus 34:7)."

44. [The students asked,] "And why is it that chirik is of the language of burning?" [He responded,] "Because it is a fire that burns all fires, as is written, 'And the fire of YHVH descended and consumed the offering, the wood, the stone, and the dust, and it licked up the water that was in the trench.' (1 Kings 18:38)."

45. Mar [Rachumai] said, "As it is written, 'And all the nation saw the voices [hakolot — often translated as 'thunder'] and the lightning' (Exodus 20:15). Can voices be seen?!? Rather, all the nation saw the voices — those [seven] voices about which David spoke. 
[1] 'A voice [kol] of YHVH is upon the waters; the God of Glory thunders, YHVH' (Psalms 29:3). 
[2] 'A voice of YHVH in strength' (Psalms 29:4), and it says, With the strength of My hand, I have done [it]' (Isaiah 10:13), and it says, 'My own hand founded the earth' (Isaiah 48:13). 
[3] 'A voice of YHVH in majesty [hadar] ' (Psalms 29:4), and the text said, 'Glorious [hod] and majestic [hadar] are His deeds, and His righteousness [tzedek] stands forever.' (Psalms 101:3). 
[4] 'A voice of YHVH shatters cedars' (Psalms 29:5). This is the bow that shatters cypress trees and cedar trees. 
[5] 'A voice of YHVH kindles flames of fire'(Psalms 29: 7). This is the one who makes peace between water and fire, that cuts the strength of the fire and prevents it from licking up the water and prevents them from evaporating it. 
[6] 'A voice of YHVH convulses the desert' (Psalms 29:8), as is said, 'And He does kindness [chesed] to His annointed, to David, and to his descendents forever [may be reading hyperliterally: and to seed him, to the world]' (Psalms 18:51), —moreso than the desert. 
[7] 'A voice of YHVH causes hinds to calve, and strips forests bare; while in the divine temple all say 'Glory!'' (Psalms 29:9). It is written, 'I adjure you, O maidens of Jerusalem, by gazelles or by hinds of the field' (Song of Songs 2:7). 
Thus you have learned that with seven voices the Torah was given. With all of them, the Lord of the World was revealed to them, and they saw Him. As it is written, 'And all the nation saw the voices' (Exodus 20:15)."

46. [Mar Rachumai continued,] "One text says, 'He bent the heavens and descended, a thick cloud below His feet' (2 Samuel 22:10), another text says, 'He came down upon Mount Sinai to the top of the mountain' (Exodus 19:20), and another text says, 'For from the heavens I spoke with you' (Exodus 20:19). How is this to be understood? His great fire was on the land, which was one voice, and the remainder of the voices were in the heavens, as is written 'From the heavens, you could hear His voice to instruct you; on the earth, you could see the great fire, and you heard His words from within the fire' (Deuteronomy 4:36). What is this great [fire]? And from where was the speech coming out? From within the fire, as it is said 'and you heard His words from within the fire' ?"

47. [Mar Rachumai continued,] "And what is, 'And an image you did not see, only a voice' (Deuteronomy 4:12)? This is as Moses said to Israel, 'For you did not see all the image' (Deuteronomy 4:15) — [you saw] an image but not the whole image. A parable, to what it is may be compared: To a king that stands above his servants, draped in a white robe. It's not enough for the awe of the kingdom that they look at his garments. Further, the king was too far away, but they heard his voice. Can they see his throat? You would say, 'No.' Thus, you learned that they saw an image but not the whole image. Thus, it is written, 'And an image you did not see, only a voice' and it is written, 'The voice [i.e., sound] of words you heard' (Deuteronomy 4:12)."

48. [Mar Rachumai continued,] "One text says, 'And all the people saw the voices' (Exodus 20:15), and another text says, 'You heard the voice of words' (Deuteronomy 4:12). How can this be? At first, 'they saw the voices.' What did they see? Seven voices about which David spoke. And at the end, they heard the speech that comes out from between them all. But have we not learned that [that they are] ten? The rabbis said that all of them [the commandments] were spoken in one utterance (BT Shevuot 20b). So too, all of them were spoken in one utterance. But there were seven utterances! There were seven voices, and regarding three of them, it is said, 'You heard the voice of words, but you did not see an image — only a voice' (Deuteronomy 4:12). Thus, you learned that all of them were spoken as one utterance, so that Israel would not err by saying that another had helped him or the angels [helped] as His voice alone could not be this strong. Because of this, He returned and included all of them."

49. [Mar Rachumai continued,] "Another interpretation: So that the world would not say, 'Since there are ten utterances [ma'amarot], corresponding to ten kings, perhaps they cannot speak with one mouth,' it is written "I am" (Exodus 20:2), and all ten are included in it. What are ten kings? Seven voices and three sayings [amarim]. And what are 'sayings'? 'And YHVH affirmed you [he'emir'cha] today' (Deuteronomy 26:18). And what are these three? As is written, 'The beginning of wisdom [chochmah] is — acquire wisdom; with all your acquisitions, acquire understanding [binah]' (Proverbs 4:7); as it is said, 'The soul of Shaddai gives them understanding' (Job 32:8) — the soul of Shaddai, She gives them understanding. What is the third? As the old man says to the child, 'Seek not things concealed from you, nor search those hidden from you. Reflect on that which is permitted to you; you have no business with secret matters' (BT Chagigah 13a)."

50. [Mar Rachumai continued,] it was taught, ' It is the glory of Elohim to conceal a word' (Proverbs 25:2). What is 'a word'? As it is said, 'Truth is the essence of Your word' (Psalms 119:160) [and] 'The glory of kings is to examine a word' (Proverbs 25:2). What is 'a word'? As is written, 'A word spoken in its proper form [אופניו]' (Proverbs 25:11). Do not read 'proper form' [אופניו], rather, 'His faces' [אפניו]."

51. His students asked Rabbi Berachiah, "Accept our words before you!" He did not give them permission. One time, he did give them permission, but he* did this to check whether now they could direct their souls. "The first day," he examined them and said, "Let me hear your wisdom." 
They opened and said, "'In the beginning' (Genesis 1:1) is one. [Two is:] 'Spirit that will wrap itself is from before me; souls I made' (Isaiah 57:16). [Three is:] 'The brook [פלג] of Elohim is filled with water' (Psalms 65:10)." 
[Rabbi Berachiah asked them,] "What is 'brook'?"
[They answered,] "You taught us, our rabbi,** that [God] took waters of creation and divided them, gave half in the heavens and half in the ocean sea, as is written, 'The brook of Elohim is filled with water' — and through it, a person learns Torah. As rabbi Chama said, 'Through the merit of gemilut chasadim a person learns Torah. Similarly, it is written, All who are thirsty, go to water, and if he has no money [כסף], go, obtain food and eat' (Isaiah 55:1) — go to Him and He will bestow kindness to you: obtain*** and eat!'"
*[Ed. - Sefaria text reads "she," but other versions say, "he."]
**[Ed. - Sefaria text reads "we saw"; other versions of the text say "our rabbi."]
***[alternatively, 'hope.' For example, see Nehemiah 2:13, 15; Psalms 119:166, 145:15; Esther 9:1]

52. [Rabbi Berachiah responded,] “Another interpretation: ’And if he has no silver [כסף], go to him’ (Isaiah 55:1) — [go to Him,] because He has silver! As is written, ’To me is the silver and to me is the gold’ (Chaggai 2:8). What is ’Mine is the silver and Mine is the gold’? A parable, to which this is similar: to a king who has two treasure houses, one of silver and one of gold. The name of the silver was in his right and that of gold was in his left. He said, ‘This [the silver] will be available and easy to bring out.’ He makes his words gentle, and cleaves to the poor, and directs them with calm, as is written, ’Your right hand, YHVH, glorious in strength’ (Exodus 15:6). And if one is happy with his portion — good — but if not, ’Your right hand, YHVH shatters the enemy’ (Exodus 15:6)."
[The students asked,] "What is ’Your right hand, YHVH shatters the enemy’?” 
He said to them, “This is the gold, as is written, ’Mine is the silver and Mine is the gold.’”

53. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "And why is it called gold [זהב]? Since three traits are included in it: 
1) male, which is zayin; 
2) the soul [נשמה], which is the heh and [includes] the five [ה] names of the soul, ruach [רוח], chayah [חיה], yechidah [יחידה], nefesh [נפש], and neshama [נשמה] — why was she [the soul] made in five? To be a throne to the zayin, as is written, 'For a higher one is protected by a higher one' (Ecclesiastes 5:7) — and, 
3) the bet establishes them, as is said, In [ב] the beginning, created' (Genesis 1:1)."

54. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "And why was she created thusly? A parable, to which this is similar: to a king who had a beautiful and nice daughter, pleasant and complete, and he married her to the son of a king. He dressed, crowned, and adorned her, and gave her to him with a great dowry. Is it possible for the king to dwell outside his house [i.e., with her in her new house]? You would say, 'No.' Is it possible for him to sit all the day with her always? You would say, 'No.' How is [he to deal with] this? He placed a window between him and her, and every time that the daughter needed her father or the father needed his daughter, they would meet together by the window. As is written, 'All the glory of the daughter of a king is inside her, her dress is embroidered with gold' (Psalms 45:14)."

55. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "And what is Bet? As is written at the end, 'With wisdom, a house is built [יבנה]' (Provers 24:3) — 'was built' [נבנה] is not said, rather, 'he will build' [יבנה]. In the future, the Blessed Holy One will build her and adorn her two–thousandfold, beyond what she was. As we have said concerning what it was when the Torah began with Bet, it is written, 'I was with Him as an artisan, and I was a source of delight every day [יום יום]' (Proverbs 8:30) — two thousand years, that she was first [ראשית]." 
[They asked him,] "Two? But doesn't the text say seven? As is written, 'The light of the moon will be like the light of the sun, and the light of the sun will be sevenfold' (Isaiah 30:26).*
[He replied,] "As we say, 'Just as the sun is to seven, so to, the moon is to seven.' Rather, we say, 'two thousand'."**
*[Ed. - some manuscripts include: 'Like the light of the seven days' of creation [בראשית]]
**[Ed. - some manuscripts show "rather" ’[אלא] as a possibility instead of "I" [אנא] and "we say" [אמרינן] instead of "they said" [אמרו]. While this alternative is not universal, it makes more sense.]

56. They [the students] said to him [Rabbi Berachiah], "Until here, five. From here and onwards, what?" 
He said to them, "I will explain gold first. Why gold? It teaches that judgment comes out from it. And if you incline your words the right or the left, you will be admonished by it."

57. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "Why is it written, 'The light of the moon will be like the light of the sun, and the light of the sun will be sevenfold, like the light of the seven days [שבעת הימים]' (Isaiah 30:26). 'The days [הימים]'? It should have said 'days [ימים]'! But these are the days about whom it is written, 'Because YHVH made six days' (Exodus 31:17)." 
His friend said to him, "This, I have received from what is written, 'Because YHVH made six days', like that which you said, 'The Blessed Holy One made six beautiful vessels.' What are they?"
[He continued,] 'The Heavens and the Earth' (Exodus 31:17, Genesis 1:1)."
[His friend objected,] "But they are seven!"
[He continued,] "And therefore, it is written, 'On the seventh day, [God] stopped and rested [וינפש]' (Exodus 31:17). What is 'rested [וינפש]'? It teaches that the day of Shabbat establishes all souls [הנפשות], as is said, 'and [He] ensouled [וינפש].'"

58. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "Another interpretation: It teaches that souls fly out from there, as it is said, 'and [He] souled [וינפש]' (Exodus 31:17) until the thousandth generation, as is said, 'A word commanded to the thousandth generation' (Psalms 105:8). And next to it, 'That was cut with Abraham' (Psalms 105:9). What is 'that was cut'? [God] cut him a covenant between the ten fingers of his hand and then toes of his foot. Abraham was shamed. The Blessed Holy One said to him, 'Here, I have covenanted with you' (Genesis 17:4) and with it, you will be 'The father of a great nation' (Genesis 17:4)"

59. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "And what is 'heavens'? It teaches that the Blessed Holy One mixed together fire and water, measured them this with this, and made from them a beginning of His words. As is written, 'The beginning of your words is truth' (Psalms 119:160). Thus, it is written, 'heavens [שמים]' — 'placed water' [שם מים] — fire and water." 
They said to him, "Until here, 'The One Who makes peace in His heights' (Job 25:2). He gave peace and love between them, and He will give peace and love between us."

60. [Rabbi Berachiah continued,] "Further, we have said, 'Seven in a day, I praise you for the justice of your righteousness' (Psalms 119:164)." 
They said to him, "What are they?"
He said, "You did not examine my words. Go out and examine them, and you will find them."

61. They asked him, "What is Tzadi?"
He said to them, "A Nun [and] a Yod is Tzadi. His partner is also a Nun [and] a Yod. This is as it is written, 'The righteous [צדיק] is the foundation of the earth' (Proverbs 10:25)."

62. They asked him, "Why is it written, 'He took him [Bilaam] to Sedeh-Tzofim [שדה צופים]' (Numbers 23:14)? What is Sedeh-Tzofim [שדה צופים]? [And] it is written, 'Come my beloved, let us go out to the field [השדה]' (Song of Songs 7:12). What is 'the field'? 
[He answered,] "Do not say 'the field,' rather 'the chest [השידה].'"* 
[They asked,] "And what is the chest?" 
[He replied,] "The Blessed Holy One's heart said to Him. 'Come my beloved, let us go out to the chest [השידה],' to journey and not remain in one place."
[Ed. - manuscripts and commentators read השידה not השירה. The word השידה ordinarily denotes a chest, box, or cabinet, not the anatomical chest.]

63. [The students asked,] "And what is His heart?"
He said to them, "If so, Ben Zoma is from outside, and you are with him. A heart [לב] is 32, and they were hidden, and with them, the world was created." 
[They asked,] "What is 32?"
He said to them, "Thirty-two paths. A parable of a king who was in a room of rooms, and the count of the rooms was 32. To every room, there was a path. Is it fitting for the king to bring everyone into his room through the way of his paths? You would said, 'No.' Is it fitting for him to reveal his innerness, compartments, hiddenness, concealedness, and desires? You would say, 'No.' What did he do? He touched the daughter and included in her all the paths and garments. Those who want to enter into his presence will examine there. He married her to a king and also gave him a gift. Sometimes he calls her 'my sister,' in his love for her, since they were from one place. Sometimes he calls her his daughter, because she is his daughter. Sometimes, he calls her 'my mother.'"

64. [He continued,] "And further, since there is no judgment if there is no wisdom, as indeed, it is said, 'YHVH gave wisdom to Solomon' (1 Kings 5:26), and afterwards he judged his judgment by his measure. As it is said, 'When all Israel heard the decision that the king had rendered, they stood in awe of the king; for they saw that he possessed divine wisdom to execute justice' (1 Kings 3:28)."*
*[Ed. - Sefer Bahir has two different section-numbering systems. Here, Sefaria includes לא as a word in the text, which looks like the word "no." However, this is actually the number 31, from the alternate numeration.]

65. [He continued,] "And what wisdom did the Blessed Holy One give to Solomon? Solomon carried the name of the Blessed Holy One, as we have said that every 'Solomon' that is said in Song of Songs it he Holy One except one (BT Shevuot 35b). The Blessed Holy One said, 'Since your name is like the name of My glory, I will marry my daughter to you. But she is married! Say that as a gift, He gave her to him, as is written, 'YHVH gave wisdom to Solomon (1 Kings 5:26) — but it was not given. When was it given? Further on, as is said, 'For they saw the wisdom of Elohim within him to execute justice' (1 Kings 3:28). Therefore, say that the wisdom that Elohim gave to him — and that was with him in his rooms — was in him to execute justice. What is 'to execute justice'? [It teaches us] that the wisdom of Elohim is within a person, every time he executes justice. This is the one that helps him and brings him close. And if not, it sends him away. And further, it disciplines him, as is written 'I will discipline you; I am anger' (Leviticus 26:28).*
*[Ed. - A more standard translation would be 'I will discipline you, I too'.]

66. And Rabbi Rachumai said, "Why is it writtern, 'I too,' (Leviticus 26:28)? The Blessed Holy One said, 'I will discipline you,' and Knesset Yisrael said, 'Do not silently-imagine that I seek compassion for you. Rather, I will reprimand you. It is not enough that I will adjudicate the case,* rather, I will also punish** you.'"
*["Adun hadin" — could also read "Adon hadin," "Master of judgment."]
**["Reprimand"]

67. [He continued,] "What is 'Seven upon your sins' (Leviticus 26:28)? Knesset Yisrael said, 'I will reprimand you, I and also them,' as is said about them, 'I praise You seven' (Psalms 119:164). They joined her, answered, and said, 'Also the seven of us. Even though among us is the one who turns about, the one who oversees the merit and goodness, we too are turned and will reprimand.'Why? Because of 'your sins'. 'But if you return to Me, I will return to you, as is written, 'Return to me and I will return to you' (Malachi 3:7). 'I will return' is not written, rather 'I will return Myself to you'* — with you — and all of us will seek compassion from the King.' And what does the King say? 'Return, rebellious children, I will heal your afflictions' (Jeremiah 3:22) [and] 'Return and cause returning' [שובו והשיבו] (Ezekiel 18:30). What is 'cause returning'? Return and seek the seven that will return with you, as is written 'cause returning' them that say, 'Seven upon your sins'' (Leviticus 26:28).
*[Ed. - Some versions read ואשוב/ואשובה, matching Malachi 3:7, “I will return to you.” Sefaria brings another version, apparently the version possessed by the author of Or HaGanuz, which appears to read ואשיב/ואשיבה as Hifil, “I will restore/return [them] to you,” and explains the unstated object as the seven lower middot, which return with Israel through repentance. This reading shifts the passage from divine accompaniment to sefirotic restoration.]

68. His students asked Rabbi Rachumai, "Why is it written, 'A prayer of Habakkuk the propphet, on Shigionot' (Habbakkuk 3:1)?" 
[He answered,] "Prayer [תפלה] — it should have said 'praise' [תהלה]! But everyone who turns his heart from the toils of the world and gazes into the workings of the chariot is received before the Blessed Holy One as if he had prayed the entire day, as it is said, 'prayer'. What is shigyanot’ [שגיונות]? As it is written, 'Stray [תשגה] in your love of her always' (Proverbs 5:19). And what is this? The workings of the chariot, as is written, 'YHVH, I have heard of Your renown; I am awed' (Habakkuk 3:2)."

69. [Rabbi Rachumai continued,] "Another matter: Why is it written, 'YHVH, I have heard of Your renown; I am awed' (Habakkuk 3:2)? Why is it that when he said, 'Your renown', he said, 'I am awed', but when he said, 'Within years' (Habakkuk 3:2), he did not say, 'I am awed'? It was from 'Your renown' that 'I am awed'. What is 'Your renown'? A place that hears him. And why did he say, 'I have heard [שמעתי]'? It should have said, 'I understood'. As was written, 'A nation whose language you do not understand [תשמע]' (Deuteronomy 28:49)."

70. [Rabbi Rachumai continued,] "And why does it say, 'I am awed' (Habakkuk 3:2)? Because an ear is in the image of Aleph, and Aleph is the head of all letters. Further, Aleph causes all of the other letters to stand, and Aleph is the image of the brain. Just as Aleph, when you pronounce her, you open your mouth, so too, thought, when you think toward endlessness and boundlessness.* From the Aleph, all the letters come out. Do you not see that she is the first of them? And it says, 'YHVH is at their head' (Micah 2:13). It establishes for us that every name that is written with Yod He Vav He is set apart [מיוחד] for the Blessed Holy One and sanctified in holiness. What is in holiness? The holy palace. And where is the holy palace? You must say: in thought. And this is Aleph, as is written, 'YHVH, I have understood** Your renown; I am awed' (Habakkuk 3:2).
[Ed. - more literally, "toward no end or limit."]
**[Ed. - see previous, #69.]

71. [Rabbi Rachumai continued,] "This Habakkuk said, 'I prayed; I know that it was received with delight. I was also delighted, and when I arrived at such-and-such a place, and understood, I heard of Your renown, and I was awed.'* Therefore, 'Renew Your deeds in the midst of years' (Habakkuk 3:2) — in Your uniqueness. A parable to which it can it be compared: to a king — faithful, wonderous, and concealed — who enters his house and commands that none will seek him. Because of this, the one who seeks (him) will be afraid, lest the king will know that he trespassed his [the king's] command. Thus, he says, 'I feared [יראתי].' 'YHVH, revive Your work in the midst of years’ — thus Habakkuk said, 'Since Your Name is thus, and in You is Your Name, ‘Renew Your deeds in the midst of years.’ So may it be forever.”
*[Ed. - This is not a direct quotation from the book of Habakkuk.]

72. [Rabbi Rachumai continued,] "Another interpretation: 'Renew Your deeds in the midst of years' (Habakkuk 3:2) — a parable to which it can be compared: to a king that had a good jewel, and she was the desire of his kingdom. At the time when he is happy, he embraces her, kisses her, places her on his head, and loves her. Habakkuk said, 'Even though the angels are with You, this desired gem — she is in the world!' Thus, 'Renew in the midst of years'. What is the meaning of the 'years'? As it is said, 'And Elohim said, 'Let there be light'' (Genesis 1:3). There is no light except day, as is written, 'The great light to rule the day and the small light to rule the night' (Genesis 1:15). Years are [made] from days, as it is written, 'Renew in the midst of years' — within this gem, who births the years."

73. [The students objected,] "But is it not written, 'From the east, I will bring your seed' (Isaiah 43:5)? The sun rises in the east, and you say that the gem is the day!"
[Rabbi Rachumai replied,] "I did not say this, rather, 'It was evening, and it was morning, day' (Genesis 1:5). As it is written, 'In the day, YHVH Elohim made earth and heaven' (Genesis 2:4)."

74. [The students objected,] "But is it not written, '[God] set darkness as His hiding place, His pavilion around Him, darkness of waters, thick clouds of the skies' (Psalms 18:12)?"
He [Rabbi Rachumai] said to them, "It is written about Him, 'The Heavens will rain down righteousness [צדק]' (Isaiah 45:8). This righteousness is the treat of judgment [דין] of the world, as is said, 'Justice, justice, you will pursue' (Deuteronomy 16:20), and it is written after it, 'In order that you will live and inherit the earth' (Ibid.). If you will judge yourself, you will live. If not, He will judge you and enact [the judgment] against your will."

75. [The students asked,] "And why is 'Tzedek, tzedek [צדק]' (Deuteronomy 16:20) — two times?"
[Rabbi Rachumai] said to them, "As is written, 'From the brilliance before Him' (Psalms 18:13). The first 'tzedek' is actual [ממש] Tzedek, and She is Shechinah, as is written, 'Righteousness [צדק] dwells in Her' (Isaiah 1:21)."
[They asked,] "And what is the second 'tzedek'?"
[He answered,] "This is tzedek that frightens the righteous [צדיקים]." 
[They asked,] "And is this Tzedek Tzedakah or not?"
[He answered,] "No."
"Why?"
"As is written, 'He dons Tzedakah like armor' (Isaiah 59:17) and Tzedek is 'A helm of redemption on His head' (Ibid.). There is no 'His head' other than Truth [אמת], as it is said, 'The head of Your words is Truth' (Psalms 119:160), and there is no Truth other than Peace [שלום], as is said, 'For He will be Peace and Truth in my days' (Isaiah 39:8). Is it possible for a person to say this? Thus, Hezekiah said [that] this trait that You gave to David, my father, is a half day, and peace and truth are half days. Therefore, he mentioned 'my days,' and he mentioned 'peace and truth' and 'in my days', as everything is one, as is written, peace 'There was evening and there was morning — one day' Genesis 1:5). What is the day? Peace, so too he seeks peace as is said 'Peace and truth he will be in my days' (Genesis 42:19). With this trait that you gave to David, my father, as is written, 'His throne, as the sun, before him' (Psalms 89:37)."

76. [The students asked,] "And what is, 'Make it known within these years' (Habakkuk 3:2)?"
[Rabbi Rachumai responded,] "Thus he said, 'I know that you are the Holy God [אל], as is written, 'Who is like You, majestic in holiness' (Exodus 15:11) — holiness in You and You in holiness — and despite this (???), 'make it known within these years'."
[They asked,] "What is 'make it known'?"
[He replied,] "You will have compassion, as it is said, 'Elohim looked at the children of Israel, and Elohim knew [them]' (Exodus 2:25). What is 'He knew'? A paraple, to which it is simliar: to a king that can a beautiful wife and he established children up from her, loved them, and raised them. They went out into evil ways, and he hated them and hated their mother. Their mother returned to them and said, 'My children, why are you doing thusly, that your father hates me and you?' Until they were comforted and returned to do the will of their father. Their father saw this, loved them as at first, and remembered their mother, as is written 'And God saw...' and 'he knew', and it is written, 'Make it known within these years'"

77. [The students asked,] "And what is 'Though angry, may You remember love' (Habakkuk 3:2)?"
[Rabby Rachumai] said, "At the time that they sin against you, my child, and you are angry with them, remember love."
[They asked,] "But what is 'May You remember compassion'?"
[He responded,] "This is as is said, 'I love You, YHVH, my strength [חזקי]' (Psalms 18:2). And You gave to him this trait, which is the Shechinah of Israel and remembered his son, who inherited Her. And You gave to him, as is written, 'YHVH gave wisdom to Solomon' (1 Kings 5:26). He rememberes their father, Abraham, as is written, 'The seed of Abraham, my beloved' (Isaiah 41:8) — 'Within the years, make it known' (Habakkuk 3:2)."

78. [The students asked,] "From where is it that Abraham had a daughter?" 
[Rabbi Rachumai responded,] "As it is written, 'YHVH blessed Abraham with everything [בכל]' (Genesis 24:1), and it is written, 'All who are called by My name and who, for My Glory, I created, I formed him, indeed, I made him' (Isaiah 43:7)." 
[They asked,] "Was this blessing his daughter or not?"
[He replied,] "Yes, she was his daughter. A parable to which it can be compared: to a king that had a servant, perfect and complete, before him. He tested him with many tests, and he withstood all of them. The king said, 'What will I give to this servant, and what will I do for him?' He said, 'I do not have anything, but I will command my older brother to advise, protect, and honor him.' The servant returned with his his older brother and learned his traits. The brother loved him greatly and called him his love, as is written, 'The seed of Abraham, my beloved' (Isaiah 41:8). He said, 'What will I give to him, and what will I do for him?' Behold, I have made a beautiful vessel, and in it is a beautiful pearl. There is nothing like them amongst kings' treasures. I will give it to him and he will be worthy in his place, as is written, 'YHVH blessed Abraham with everything [בכל]' (Genesis 24:1)."

79. [Rabbi Rachumai continued,] "Another interpretation: 'I have heard of Your renown; I am awed' (Habakkuk 3:2). [Meaning,] 'I understood Your renown; I am awed.' What did he understand, such that he was awed? He understood the thought of the Blessed Holy One. Just as there is no end to the thought of a person, which can carry to the end of the world, so too, the ear does not have an end and is not sated, as is written, 'The ear is not filled from hearing' (Ecclesiastes 1:8). Why is [the letter] Zayin in ear [אוזן]? Why? Because the ear is in the image of [the letter] Aleph, and Aleph> is essence of the ten utterances. Thus, 'the ear is not filled from hearing.'"

80. [The students asked,] "But why (WHAT IS?) Zayin"?
[Rabbi Rachumai responded,] "As it is written in ear (WAS THIS IN THE QUESTION OR ANSWER? KAPLAN SAYS IT'S IN Q]. That we have said, all that the Blessed Holy One brings into His world, its name is from its essence, as is written, 'Whatever the Adam called each living creature [נפש חיה], that was its name' (Genesis 2:19) — that is to say, its body was thus. Where do we learn that one's name becomes its body? As is written, 'The name of a righteous person is invoked in blessing, but the name of the wicked rots' (Proverbs 10:7). Does a name rot?* Rather, it means the body. Here too, [it means] his body."

83. And what is Nun? To teach you that the brain is the essence of the spinal cord, and from there it draws continually. Were it not for the spinal cord, the brain could not endure, and without the brain the body could not endure, for the whole body exists for the sake of the brain. And if the whole body did not endure, the brain would not endure. Therefore the spinal cord pours out to the whole body from the brain, and this is the bent Nun. But this [other] Nun is long. The long Nun is always at the completion of a word, to teach you that the long Nun includes both the bent and the long; but the bent [Nun] is foundation — to teach you that the long Nun is composed of male and female.

84. The open Mem — what is the open Mem? It is composed of male and female. The closed Mem — what is it? It is made like a belly from above. And Rabbi Rechumai said, the belly is like [the letter] Tet. That one said "like a Tet on the inside"; I say "like a Tet on the outside."

85. And what is Mem? Do not read "Mem" but "mayim" (water). Just as this water is moist, so too the belly is always moist. And why is the lesser Mem [the open one] composed of male and female, while the closed one is male? To teach you that the essence of the Mem is male, and the open one was added for the sake of the female. And just as the male does not give birth through an opening, so too the closed Mem does not give birth except through the open one; and just as the female gives birth through an opening, so too [is] the Mem, closed and open.

86. And why did you see fit to include the Mem in both open and closed [forms]? Because we say, do not read "Mem" but "mayim" (water), and the woman is cold, therefore she needs to be warmed by the male. And why did you see fit to include the Nun in both bent and long [forms]? As it is written (Psalms 72:17), "Before the sun his name shall be Yinnon" — with two Nuns, a bent Nun and a long Nun; and it must be through male and female.

87. It is written (Ecclesiastes 1:8), "the ear is not filled with hearing," and it is written (ibid.), "nor is the eye satisfied with seeing." This teaches that both of them draw from Thought (Machashavah). And what is Thought? A king whom all that was created — in the upper and the lower [worlds] — needs.

88. And why is it that we say [it] "arose in Thought," and we do not say "descended," seeing that we say, "One who gazes upon the vision of the Chariot descends and afterward ascends"? There, because we say "one who gazes upon the vision (tzefiyah) of the Chariot" — and "vision" we render as "watch" (sekhuta), and it says (Isaiah 21:8), "And he cried like a lion, upon the watchtower, O Lord" [there is a lookout]. But here, in Thought, there is no vision at all and no limit (takhlit) at all; and anything that has no limit and no end has no descent, as people say, "So-and-so descended to the depth of his fellow's understanding (da'at)" — not to the depth of his thought (machashavah).

89. Rabbi Amorai sat and expounded: What is that which is written, "segol"? Rather, its name is "segulah" (treasure), as stated above, [and it comes] after the "zarka." Why "zarka"? As its name, so it is, for it is thrown (nizrak) like a thing that is cast; and after it comes "the treasure (segulat) of kings and provinces."

90. And why "zarka"? As it is written (Ezekiel 3:12), "Blessed be the glory of the Lord from His place" — implying that none knows His place. And we say there [that] the crown ascends and comes to the head of the Owner (Koneh), as it is written (Genesis 14:19), "Owner of heaven and earth." And when it goes, it is like the "zarka," with the "segulah" after it, and it is at the head of all the letters.

91. And why is it at the end of the word and not at its beginning? To teach you that this crown ascends higher and higher. And how do we know that this crown is a precious stone, comprehensive and adorned? As it is written (Psalms 118:22), "The stone which the builders rejected has become the head cornerstone." And it ascends up to the place from which it was hewn, as it is written (Genesis 49:24), "From there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel."

92. And he further said: Why do we place blue (tekhelet) in the fringe (tzitzit), and why thirty-two [threads]? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had a beautiful garden, and it had thirty-two paths, and he set a keeper over them and made known to him alone those paths. He said to him, "Keep them and walk in them every day, and whenever you tread them you shall have peace." What did that keeper do? He set [other] keepers over them. He said, "If I am alone on these paths, it is impossible for one keeper to maintain all these paths; moreover, the world will say this king is stingy." Therefore this keeper set other keepers over all the paths. These are the thirty-two paths.

93. And why the blue? The keeper said, "Lest these keepers say the garden is ours," so he gave them a sign and said to them, "See, this is the sign of the king, that the garden is his and these paths he established, and they are not mine — and behold his seal." A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king and his daughter who had servants and wished to travel far. They feared the awe of the king; the king gave them his sign. They feared the daughter; she gave them a sign. They said, "Henceforth, with these two signs, (Psalms 121:7) 'The Lord shall guard you from all evil, He shall guard your soul.'"

94. Rabbi Amorai sat and expounded: What is that which is written (1 Kings 8:27), "Behold the heavens and the heaven of heavens cannot contain You"? This teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, has seventy-two names, and He fixed them all in the tribes, as it is written (Exodus 28:10), "six of their names on the one stone, and the names of the six that remain on the second stone, according to their generations." And it is written (Joshua 4:9), "And twelve stones he set up," etc. Just as these are stones of remembrance, so too these are stones of remembrance. And the twelve stones are seventy-two, corresponding to the seventy-two names of the Holy One, blessed be He. And why did he begin with twelve? To teach you that the Holy One, blessed be He, has twelve leaders, and in each and every one there are six powers. And what are they? The seventy-two languages.

95. And the Holy One, blessed be He, has a single tree, and in it are twelve diagonal boundaries: the northeast boundary, the southeast boundary, the eastern-upper boundary, the eastern-lower boundary, the southwest boundary, the northwest boundary, the western-upper boundary, the western-lower boundary, the southern-upper boundary, the southern-lower boundary, the northern-upper boundary, the northern-lower boundary. And they extend and go on forever and ever, and they are the arms of the world. And within them is the tree. And corresponding to all these diagonals there are overseers, and they are twelve. And also within the sphere (galgal) are twelve overseers. These, together with the diagonals, are thirty-six overseers. And each one has one [above it], as it is written (Ecclesiastes 5:7), "For one higher than the high watches." It emerges that for the eastern direction there are nine, for the western direction nine, for the northern direction nine, for the southern direction nine — and that is twelve, twelve, and twelve, which are the overseers in the Teli (Dragon), the Sphere, and the Heart, and they are thirty-six. And they are all thirty-six [derived] from thirty-six, for the power of one is in its fellow. And although there are twelve in each and every one of the three, they all cling to one another; and all thirty-six powers are found in the first, which is the Teli. If you seek them in the Sphere you find those very ones, and if you seek them in the Heart you find those very ones. Therefore each one has thirty-six, and they are all no more than thirty-six forms. And they were all completed in thirty-two — thirty-two handed to thirty-two — and four remained, and they are sixty-four forms. And from where [do we know] that thirty-two was handed to thirty-two? As it is written (ibid.), "For one higher than the high watches." If so, that is sixty-four, lacking eight of the seventy-two names of the Holy One, blessed be He. And that is what is written (ibid.), "and higher ones over them" — and they are the seven days of the week, less one. And that is what is written (ibid. 8), "and the profit of the earth is in everything, [it is] a king." And what is the profit (yitron)? The place from which the earth was hewn, and it is a profit beyond what was. And what is the profit of every thing in the world? When the people of the world are worthy to receive from its radiance, then it is a profit.

96. And what is the earth from which the heavens were hewn? It is the throne of the Holy One, blessed be He, and it is a precious stone, and it is the Sea of Wisdom. And corresponding to it is the blue in the fringe. For Rabbi Meir said, why is blue distinguished from all kinds of colors? Because blue resembles the sea, and the sea resembles the firmament, and the firmament resembles the Throne of Glory, as it is said (Exodus 24:10), "And they saw the God of Israel, and under His feet like a work of sapphire brick, and like the essence of heaven for purity." And it says (Ezekiel 1:26), "like the appearance of a sapphire stone, the likeness of a throne."

97. Rabbi Berechiah sat and expounded: What is that which is written (Exodus 25:2), "And they shall take Me an offering (terumah)"? [It means:] Raise Me up (harimu) in your prayers. And who? The one who is generous of heart to draw away from this world. Honor him, for through him I rejoice, for he knows My name; and from him it is fitting to take My offering, as it is said (ibid.), "from every man whose heart moves him you shall take My offering" — from that one who volunteers. And Rabbi Rechumai said: the righteous and the pious of Israel, who raise Me up above the whole world by their merits — and from them the Heart is sustained, and the Heart sustains them.

98. And all the holy forms are appointed over all the nations, and Israel, being holy, took the body of the tree and its heart. Just as the heart is the splendor of the fruit of the body, so too Israel took the fruit of the goodly tree. Just as the palm tree — its branches surround it and its lulav (frond) is in the middle — so too Israel took the body of this tree, which is its heart. And corresponding to the body is the spinal cord in man, which is the essence of the body. And just as this lulav [reads] "lo lev" (it has a heart), so too the heart is handed to it. And what is the heart? These are the thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom in it; so too in each path of them is a form that guards, as it is written (Genesis 3:24), "to guard the way of the tree of life."

99. And what are these forms? As it is written (ibid.), "And He placed at the east of the Garden of Eden the cherubim and the flame of the ever-turning sword, to guard the way of the tree of life." And what is "He placed at the east of the Garden of Eden"? He placed [them] in those paths that preceded that place called the Garden of Eden, and that preceded the cherubim, as it is written (ibid.), "the cherubim"; and that preceded the flame, as it is written (ibid.), "and the flame of the ever-turning sword" — that preceded. But is it not written that heaven, water, and fire preceded, as it is written (ibid. 1:6), "Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide between water and water," and it is written (ibid. 8), "And God called the firmament heaven"? And from where [do we know] that heaven is fire? As it is written (Deuteronomy 4:24), "For the Lord your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God."

100. And from where [do we know] that heaven is the Holy One, blessed be He? As it is written (1 Kings 8:32), "And You shall hear the heavens." Now, did Solomon pray to the heavens that they should hear their prayer? Rather, [he prayed to] the One whose name is called upon the heavens, as it is written (ibid. 27), "the heavens and the heaven of heavens cannot contain You." Behold, [it is] the name of the Holy One, blessed be He, and it is fire — and you said [it] preceded. Rather say: the power of those forms of that place preceded, and afterward those holy forms [came]. And what is their power? As it says (1 Samuel 2:2), "There is none holy like the Lord, for there is none besides You, and there is no Rock like our God."

101. Rabbi Berechiah sat and expounded: What is the lulav of which we spoke — "thirty-six handed to thirty-two"? And how? He said to him: there are three princes — the Teli, the Sphere, and the Heart — and each and every one is twelve. Multiply the three, and the count is thirty-six, by which the world endures, as it is written (Proverbs 10:25), "and the righteous one is the foundation of the world."

102. It was taught: there is a single pillar from earth to firmament, and its name is Righteous One (Tzaddik), named for the righteous. And when there are righteous ones in the world, it grows strong, and if not, it grows weak; and it bears the whole world, as it is written (ibid.), "the righteous one is the foundation of the world." And if it grows weak, the world cannot endure. Therefore, even if there is but one righteous one in the world, he sustains the world, as it is said, "and the righteous one is the foundation of the world." Therefore, take My offering from him first, and afterward "and this is the offering which you shall take from them" — from the remainder. And what is it? Gold, and silver, and copper.

103. Another interpretation of "And they shall take Me an offering": and they shall take, to sanctify, the offering which is Yod, and it is the tenth. And from where [do we know] that the tenth is holy? As it is written (Leviticus 27:32), "the tenth shall be holy to the Lord." And what is holy? As it is written (Ezekiel 44:30), "and the first of all the first-fruits of all, and every offering of all," etc. And it is written (Psalms 111:10), "the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord" — do not read "the fear of the Lord" but "and the fear of the Lord."

104. His disciples asked Rabbi Eliezer our master: What is that which is written (Exodus 13:2), "Sanctify to Me every firstborn"? Is then the Holy One, blessed be He, a firstborn? He said to them: "Sanctify to Me every firstborn" means nothing but "second to the Holy," and it is called by the name of Israel, as it is written (ibid. 4:22), "My son, My firstborn, Israel." As it were, He was with them at the time of the bondage, and that is what is written (Exodus 4:23), "Send out My son that he may serve Me" — and not "My firstborn." Rabbi Rechumai said: What is that which is written (Deuteronomy 22:7), "You shall surely send away the mother, and the young you shall take for yourself" — and it does not say "you shall surely send away the father"? Rather, "you shall surely send away the mother" is out of honor for that one who is called the Mother of the World, as it is said (Proverbs 2:3), "for if you call out to understanding (binah)."

105. And what is "and the young you shall take for yourself" (Deuteronomy, ibid.)? Rabbi Rechumai says: those young ones that she raised. And what are they? The seven days of Creation and the seven days of Sukkot — and that is the seven days of the week, which are seven. Is there [something] among them that is more holy, as it is written of them (Leviticus 23:37), "holy convocations"? And if so, is the Atzeret [Eighth Day] a holy convocation (ibid. 36)? He said to him: yes, but this [Atzeret] is one and that [Sukkot] is [as] two, as it is written (ibid. 35), "on the first day a holy convocation," and "on the seventh day a holy convocation." He said to him: why is Atzeret one? Because on it the Torah was given to Israel; and when the Torah — the beginning — was created, the Holy One, blessed be He, ruled in His world alone with it, as it is written (Psalms 111:10), "the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord." He said: since it is so, your holiness shall be for you alone. And what is Sukkot? He said to him: [the letter] Bet, as it is written (Proverbs 24:3), "By wisdom a house is built." And from where [do we know] that Sukkot is a house? As it is written (Genesis 33:17), "And Jacob journeyed to Sukkot and built himself a house, and for his cattle he made booths (sukkot); therefore he called the name of the place Sukkot."

106. Rabbi Berechiah sat and expounded: What is the Teli? This is a likeness that is before the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is written (Song of Songs 5:11), "his locks are curled (taltalim)." What is the Sphere (galgal)? This is the belly. And what is the Heart? As it is written (Deuteronomy 4:11), "unto the heart of heaven"; and in it are included the thirty-two wondrous paths of Wisdom.

107. What is that which is written (Numbers 6:24–26), "May the Lord bless you and keep you; may the Lord make His face shine upon you and be gracious to you; may the Lord lift up His face to you and grant you peace"? This is the explicit name of the Holy One, blessed be He, and it is the name of twelve letters, as it is written [thrice] "YHVH, YHVH, YHVH." This teaches that the names of the Holy One, blessed be He, are three hosts, and each and every host resembles its fellow, and its name is like His name; and they are all sealed with Yod-He-Vav-He. And how do you combine Yod-He-Vav-He twenty-four times? That is one host — "may the Lord bless you"; and the second, "may the Lord make His face shine" — and these are twenty-four names of the Holy One, blessed be He; and the third, "may the Lord lift up" — and these are [again] twenty-four names of the Holy One, blessed be He. This teaches that each host, its heads and its princes are twenty-four. Multiply twenty-four three times, and they are the seventy-two names of the Holy One, blessed be He. And that is the seventy-two [name] that emerges from (Exodus 14:19–21) "and journeyed... and came... and stretched out."

108. And who are these princes? This teaches that there are three: for Gevurah (Might) is prince over all the holy forms from the left side of the Holy One, blessed be He, and it is Gabriel; and from my right, a prince over all the holy forms, and it is Michael; and in the middle is Truth, and it is Uriel, prince over all the holy forms. And each prince has twenty-four forms, and there is no counting of his legions, as it is written (Job 25:3), "Is there a number to His legions?" If so, that is seventy-two and seventy-two? He said to him: no, for at the time that Israel offer a sacrifice before their Father in heaven, they are united together, and that is the Oneness of our God.

109. And why is it called an offering (korban)? Rather, because it draws near (mekarev) the holy forms and powers, as it is written (Ezekiel 37:17), "and bring them near, one to one, into one tree for yourself, and they shall be as one in your hand." And it says "for a pleasing aroma," and there is no aroma except through the nostril, and there is no breath — which is the aroma — except through the nostril; and there is no "pleasing (nichoach)" except descent, as it is written (Leviticus 9:22), "and he came down (vayered)," which we render "and he descended." And the spirit descends and unites with those holy forms and is drawn near through the sacrifice — and that is why it is called an offering.

110. The name that emerges from the three verses "and journeyed... and came... and stretched out" (Exodus 14:19–21): the letters of the first verse, "and journeyed," are set into the name in the order of the verse; the letters of the second verse are set into the name in reverse of the verse; and the letters of the third verse, "and stretched out," are set into the name in the order of the verse, like the first, "and journeyed." And in each and every one of the verses there are seventy-two letters. It emerges that in each and every name of the seventy-two names that emerge from these three verses — "and journeyed... and came... and stretched out" — there are three letters. These are the seventy-two names that emerge, and they are divided into three parts, twenty-four to each part; and over each part of the three parts is a prince high above them; and each part has four directions to guard — east and west, north and south — and they are divided, six to each direction. It emerges that for the four directions there are twenty-four forms, and so for the second, and so for the third; and they are all sealed with "YHVH, God of Israel, living God, Shaddai, high and exalted, dwelling forever, and holy is His name" — YHVH, blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.

111. Rabbi Ahilai sat and expounded: What is that which is written, "The Lord reigns, the Lord has reigned, the Lord shall reign forever and ever"? Rather, this is the explicit name concerning which permission was given to combine and to mention it, as it is written (Numbers 6:27), "and they shall place My name upon the children of Israel, and I will bless them." And it is a name of twelve letters, in the name of the priestly blessing, "may the Lord bless you," etc., which are three [phrases] and are twelve [letters]; and their vocalization is thus: yif'al, yif'ol, yif'ul. And whoever guards it and mentions it in holiness and purity — all his prayers are accepted; and not only that, but he is beloved below and beloved above, cherished below and cherished above, and answered and helped at once. This is the explicit name written upon the forehead of Aaron; and by the explicit name of seventy-two letters; and by the explicit name of twelve names which the Holy One, blessed be He, handed to Masmariah, who stands before the curtain (pargod), and he handed them to Elijah on Mount Carmel, and through them he ascended and did not taste the taste of death.

112. And these are the explicit names, precious and glorious, which are twelve, for the twelve tribes of Israel: Ahatzitzahron, Achlitahron, Shemaktron, Damushahron, Vetzaftzafsitahron, Hormiron, Barchiahron, Ar"sh, Gadran, Basavah, Manahon, Chazhoviyah, Hohiri, Ha"h, Ehyeh, Vehareitahon. And they are all included in the thirty-two, and they divide into twenty-four names; and in them is included male and female, and overseers in the Teli, the Sphere, and the Heart; and they are the wellsprings of Wisdom.

113. Rabbi Rechumai sat and expounded: What are the twelve tribes of Israel? Rather, this teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, has twelve tribes. And what are they? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had a beautiful spring, and all his brothers had no other water but from that spring, and they could not bear the thirst. What did he do? He made for the spring twelve channels and named them for the names of his brothers' sons, and said: if the sons are good like their fathers, they shall be worthy and I will fill the channels; their fathers shall be sated and their sons shall drink after them. And if the sons are not worthy and do not do fitting things before me, behold, the channels stand ready for this: I will give them water on the condition that they give nothing to their sons, since they do not do My will.

114. And what is the meaning of the term "tribe" (shevet)? It is a simple thing that is not square. Why? Because it is impossible [to have] a square within another square; rather, a circle runs within a square, but a square within a square does not run.

115. And what is the circle? This is the vowel-point (nekudah) of the Torah of Moses, for they are all circular, and they are like the letters as the living soul is in the body of a man — for it is impossible for [a man] to come wherever [the soul] does not stand within him, nor to utter a word small or great without a soul like it; so [is] this vowel-point, for it is impossible for a matter small or great to be uttered without the point.

116. And every point is a circle, and every letter is a square, and the letter is established by the point, and they [the points] are their life. And this point comes by way of the channels to this letter through the aroma of the sacrifice, and it descends at once, as it is written (Leviticus 1:9), "a pleasing aroma to the Lord" — that it descends to the Lord. That is what is written (Deuteronomy 6:4), "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is One."

117. Rabbi Yochanan said: What is that which is written (Exodus 15:3), "The Lord is a man of war, the Lord is His name"? Rather, "man" (ish) is a sign, as we render it: "the Lord is a man of war" — "the Lord is master, victor in the wars." And what is "master" (mari)? The first Aleph is the holy palace. The holy palace — does it enter your mind? Rather, say: the palace of the Holy.

118. Yod — by the ten utterances with which the world was created. And what are they? This is the Torah of Truth that includes all the worlds. And Shin — what is it? He said to him: the root of the tree, for the Shin is like the root of the tree.

119. And what is the tree of which you spoke? He said to him: the powers of the Holy One, blessed be He, one upon another, and they resemble a tree. Just as this tree, by means of water, brings forth fruit, so too the Holy One, blessed be He, by means of water increases the powers of the tree. And what is the water of the Holy One, blessed be He? It is Wisdom; and it is the souls of the righteous, which fly from the wellspring to the great channel and ascend and cleave to the tree. And by means of what do they fly? By means of Israel: when they are righteous and good, the Shekhinah dwells among them and dwells in their deeds, in the bosom of the Holy One, blessed be He, and makes them fruitful and multiplies them.

120. And from where [do we know] that the Shekhinah is called Righteousness (Tzedek)? As it is written (Deuteronomy 33:26), "who rides the heavens in your help, and in His majesty the skies," and it is written (Isaiah 45:8), "and let the skies pour down righteousness"; and this righteousness is the Shekhinah, as it is written (Isaiah 1:21), "righteousness lodged in her." And righteousness was given to David, as it is written (Psalms 146:10), "The Lord shall reign forever, your God, O Zion, for all generations," and it is written (1 Chronicles 11:5), "Zion, that is the city of David."

121. What is "for all generations" (dor dor)? Rabbi Papias said: "a generation goes and a generation comes" (Ecclesiastes 1:4). And Rabbi Akiva said: "a generation comes" — that has already come.

122. A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had servants, and he clothed them, according to his ability, in garments of silk and embroidery. They corrupted the order; he cast them off and thrust them from before him, and stripped them of his garments, and they went their way. He took the garments and washed them thoroughly until no stain remained in them, and set them beside him, ready. And he bought other servants and clothed them in those garments, though he did not know whether those servants were good or not. And behold, they merited the garments that had already come into the world and were worn by others before them. But the earth stands forever, and that is what is written (Ecclesiastes 12:7), "and the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to God who gave it."

123. Rabbi Amorai said: What is that which is written (Leviticus 9:22), "And Aaron lifted his hand toward the people and blessed them, and came down"? But he had already come down! Rather, "and came down" from performing the sin-offering, the burnt-offering, and the peace-offerings; and afterward "Aaron lifted his hands toward the people." Why this lifting? Because he had offered a sacrifice and was drawn near before their Father in heaven, as we said: the one who draws it near to the upper ones and unites them must unite these within their whole. And what is "the people," as it is written "toward the people"? On behalf of the people.

124. And why with the lifting of the hands (palms)? Let him bless them with a [mere] blessing! Rather, because there are ten fingers on the hands, a hint to the ten Sefirot with which heaven and earth were sealed. And those ten correspond to the Ten Commandments (lit. ten utterances), and within those ten are included the 613 commandments. And count the letters of the Ten Commandments and you will find that they are 613 letters, and in them are all twenty-two letters except for the Tet, which is not among them. Why? To teach you that the Tet is the belly, and it is not among the Sefirot.

125. And why are they called Sefirot? Because it is written (Psalms 19:2), "the heavens recount (mesaprim) the glory of God."

126. And what are they? They are three; and among them are three hosts and three dominions. The first dominion is Light, and the light of life is of water. The second dominion is the holy Chayot, the Ofanim, the wheels of the Chariot, and all the legions of the Holy One, blessed be He, who bless and revere and glorify and adorn and sanctify the King who is majestic in holiness and arrayed in the mystery of the great holy ones — a King awesome and terrible — and crown Him with three sanctifications.

127. And why three sanctifications and not four? Because the sanctification above is three by three, as it is written, "The Lord reigns, the Lord has reigned, the Lord shall reign forever and ever," and it is written (Numbers 6:24–26), "may the Lord bless... may the Lord make shine... may the Lord lift up," and it is written (Exodus 34:6), "The Lord, the Lord" — and the rest are of the attributes: what is the third "Lord"? "The Lord, God, merciful and gracious," etc. — the thirteen attributes.

128. And what is "Holy, holy, holy," and afterward "the Lord of hosts, the whole earth is full of His glory" (Isaiah 6:3)? Rather: "Holy" — the supernal crown; "Holy" — the root of the tree; "Holy" — cleaving and united in all of them; "the Lord of hosts, the whole earth is full of His glory."

129. And what is the "Holy" that cleaves and is united? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had sons, and the sons had sons. When their sons do His will, He enters among them and sustains everything and satisfies everything and bestows good upon them, so that the fathers and the sons may be sated. And when the sons do not do His will, He satisfies the fathers according to their need.

130. And what is "the whole earth is full of His glory" (ibid.)? Rather, all that earth which was created on the first day, which is above corresponding to the Land of Israel, is full of the glory of the Name. And what is it? Wisdom, as it is written (Proverbs 3:35), "the wise shall inherit glory," and it says (Ezekiel 3:12), "Blessed be the glory of the Lord from His place."

131. And what is this glory of the Lord? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had a matron (matronita) in his chamber, in whom all his hosts delighted, and she had sons, and they came every day to see the face of the king and to bless him. They said to him, "Our mother — where is she?" He said to them, "You cannot see her now." They said, "Blessed be she in every place that she is."

132. And what is that which is written (ibid.), "from His place" — implying that none knows His place? A parable: to a king's daughter who came from afar, and they did not know where she came from, until they saw that she was a woman of valor, comely and fitting in all her deeds. They said, "This one was surely taken from the side of the Light, for by her deeds the world is lit." They asked her, "From where are you?" She said, "From my place." They said, "If so, great are the people of your place; blessed be you, and blessed be her place."

133. And is this glory of the Lord not one of His hosts? It is not lessened; why do they bless it? Rather, a parable: to what is this comparable? To a man who had a beautiful garden, and outside the garden, near it, [was] a fine piece of field, [in which] he made a fine little garden. He watered the garden at the beginning of his watering, and the water went over the whole garden — but not over that piece of field, which is not attached, even though it is all one. Therefore he opened a place for it and watered it by itself.

134. Rabbi Rechumai said: Glory (Kavod) and Heart (Lev) — behold, they are one; only that the Glory is named for the action above, and the Heart is named for the action below. And that is "the glory of the Name," and that is "the heart of heaven."

135. Rabbi Yochanan said: What is that which is written (Exodus 17:11), "And it was, when Moses lifted his hand, Israel prevailed, and when he lowered his hand, Amalek prevailed"? This teaches that the world endures for the sake of the lifting of the hands. Why? Because that power which was given to Jacob our father, whose name is Israel — to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob powers were given, one to each and every one; and by the measure that each and every one walked, its like was given to him. Abraham did kindness (chesed) for the world, for he would prepare food for all who came into the world and for wayfarers, and do kindness and go out to meet them, as it is written (Genesis 18:2), "and he ran to meet them"; and further, "and he bowed to the ground" (ibid.) — this was complete kindness. And the Holy One, blessed be He, measured to him by his measure and gave him the measure of Kindness, as it is written (Micah 7:20), "You shall grant truth to Jacob, kindness to Abraham, which You swore to our fathers from days of old." What is "from days of old"? This teaches that had Abraham not done kindness and merited the measure of Kindness, Jacob would not have merited the measure of Truth; for by the merit that Abraham merited the measure of Kindness, Isaac merited the measure of Fear (Pachad), as it is written (Genesis 31:53), "and Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac." Now, is there a man who swears thus by faith in the fear of his father? Rather, up to here no power had been given to Jacob, and he swore by the power that had been given to his father, as it is said, "and Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac." And what is it? Chaos (tohu), from which the evil that confounds the sons of men goes forth. And what is it? That which is written (1 Kings 18:38), "And fire fell and consumed the burnt-offering and the wood and the stones and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench," and it is written (Deuteronomy 4:24), "For the Lord your God is a consuming fire, a jealous God."

136. And what is Kindness? It is the Torah, as it is written (Isaiah 55:1), "Ho, everyone who is thirsty, come to the water, and one who has no money" — it is money, as it is written, "come, buy and eat; and come, buy without money and without price, wine and milk." He feeds you Torah and teaches you, for you have already merited this by the merit of Abraham, who did kindness — he fed without money and gave to drink without price, wine and milk.

137. What is "wine and milk," and what has this to do with that? Rather, this teaches that the wine is Fear and the milk is Kindness. And why did he mention wine first? Because it is nearer to us. Wine and milk — does it enter your mind [literally]? Rather, say: the likeness of wine and milk. And by the merit of Abraham, who merited the measure of Kindness, Isaac merited the measure of Fear; and since Isaac merited the measure of Fear, Jacob merited the measure of Truth, which is the measure of Peace. And the Holy One, blessed be He, measured to him by his measure, as it is written (Genesis 25:27), "and Jacob was a wholesome (tam) man, dwelling in tents"; and "tam" is nothing but peace, as it is written (Deuteronomy 18:13), "you shall be wholehearted (tamim) with the Lord your God," which we render "you shall be complete (shalem)." And "tam" is nothing but Torah, as it is said (Malachi 2:6), "the Torah of truth was in his mouth," etc. And what is written after it? "in peace and uprightness he walked with Me" — and there is no uprightness (mishor) but peace, as it is written (Psalms 25:21, cf.), "wholeness (tom) and uprightness." Therefore, "and it was, when Moses lifted his hand, Israel prevailed" (Exodus 17:11) — this teaches that the measure that is called Israel has within it the Torah of Truth.

138. And what is the Torah of Truth? A thing that points to the truth of the worlds, and its action is in Thought; and it establishes the ten utterances by which the world stands, and it is one of them. And [God] created in man, corresponding to those ten utterances, ten fingers of the hands. And when Moses lifted his hand and directed, with a small measure of the heart's intent, toward that measure that is called Israel — within which is the Torah of Truth — and hinted with the ten fingers of his hands that He establishes the ten, then [it was as if to say:] if He does not help Israel, the ten utterances will not endure each and every day. Therefore, "Israel prevailed." "And when he lowered his hand, Amalek prevailed" (ibid.). Now, did Moses cause Amalek to prevail, as it is written, "and when he lowered his hand, Amalek prevailed"? Rather, a man is forbidden to keep his palms spread to the heavens for three hours.

139. His disciples asked him: to whom does one lift the palms? He said to them: to the height of heaven. From where do we [know this]? As it is written (Habakkuk 3:10), "the deep gave its voice, the height lifted its hands." From this you learn that the lifting of the palms is only to the height of heaven. And when there are among Israel wise ones who understand and know the secret of the honored Name and lift their palms, they are answered at once, as it is said (Isaiah 58:9), "then you shall call and the Lord shall answer" — if "then you shall call," the Lord answers at once.

140. What is "then" (az)? Rather, this teaches that there is no permission to call upon the Aleph by itself, but only through the two letters that cleave to it, which sit first in kingship; and with the Aleph they are three, and seven of the ten utterances remain — and that is the [numerical] seven [of the Zayin in "az"]. As it is written (Exodus 15:1), "then (az) Moses and the children of Israel sang," likewise.

141. What are the ten utterances? The first: the supernal crown, blessed and praised be His name and His people. And who is His people? Israel, as it is written (Psalms 100:3), "Know that the Lord is God; He made us, and we are His" — and to the Aleph "we [belong]," to recognize and know the One of ones, unified in all His names.

142. The second: Wisdom, as it is written (Proverbs 8:22), "The Lord acquired me at the beginning of His way, before His works of old"; and there is no "beginning" but Wisdom, as it is written (Psalms 111:10), "the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord."

143. The third: the quarry of the Torah, the treasury of Wisdom, the quarry of "the spirit of God" — this teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, hewed all the letters of the Torah and engraved them in the spirit, and made in it His forms. And that is what is written (1 Samuel 2:2), "and there is no Rock (Tzur) like our God" — and there is no Fashioner (Tzayar) like our God.

144. The third has been [given]; what is the fourth? The fourth is the righteousness of the Lord, and His merits, and His kindnesses with the whole world — and that is the right hand of the Holy One, blessed be He.

145. What is the fifth? The fifth is the great fire of the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is said (Deuteronomy 18:16), "and this great fire I will see no more, that I not die" — and it is the left hand of the Holy One, blessed be He. And what is it? They are the holy Chayot and the holy Serafim, at their right and at their left; they are the pleasant ones, high up to above, as it is written (Ecclesiastes 5:7), "and higher ones over them," and further (Ezekiel 1:18), "and their rims were high and awesome for them, and their rims were full of eyes round about for the four of them." And round about it are angels; also round about them they bow before them and kneel and say, "The Lord is God, the Lord is God."

146. The sixth: the Throne of Glory, crowned, comprehensive, praised, blessed — it is the House of the World to Come, and its place is in Wisdom, as it is written (Genesis 1:3), "And God said, let there be light, and there was light."

147. And Rabbi Yochanan said: there were two great lights, as it is said (ibid.), "and there was light," and of both of them it is said "that it was good." And the Holy One, blessed be He, took the one and stored it away for the righteous in the time to come. And that is what is written (Psalms 31:20), "how great is Your goodness which You have hidden for those who fear You, which You have wrought for those who take refuge in You," etc. This teaches that no creature can gaze upon the first light, as it is said (Genesis 1:4), "and God saw the light, that it was good," and it is written (ibid. 31), "and God saw all that He had made, and behold it was very good." The Holy One, blessed be He, saw all that He had made and saw a good, shining, radiant [light], and took of that good and included in it thirty-two paths of Wisdom and gave it to this world. And that is what is written (Proverbs 4:2), "for I have given you a good teaching (lekach); do not forsake My Torah" — that is to say, this is the treasury of the Oral Torah. And the Holy One, blessed be He, said: if they keep this measure in this world — for this measure is reckoned as part of this world, and it is the Oral Torah — they shall merit the life of the World to Come, which is the good stored up for the righteous. And what is it? The might of the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is written (Habakkuk 3:4), "and a brightness (nogah) shall be as the light" — the brightness that was taken from the first light is destined to be as the light, if the sons uphold the Torah and the commandment that I have written to instruct them (Exodus 24:12); as it is written (Proverbs 1:8), "hear, my son, the discipline of your father, and do not forsake the Torah of your mother."

148. And it is written (Habakkuk 3:4), "rays from His hand to Him, and there is the hiding of His might." What is "the hiding of His might"? Rather, that light which He hid and concealed, as it is said (Psalms 31:20), "which You have hidden for those who fear You"; and this that remained for us (ibid.), "You have wrought for those who take refuge in You" — those who take refuge in Your shadow in this world and keep Your Torah and uphold Your commandments and sanctify Your great Name and unify [it] in secret and in the open, as it is said (ibid.), "before the sons of men."

149. Rabbi Rechumai said: this teaches that she is light to Israel, and the Torah is light, as it is written (Proverbs 6:23), "for the commandment is a lamp and the Torah is light." And we say: the lamp — this is the commandment; and the light — this is the Oral Torah; and the [other] "light" — this is the Written Torah. Rather, since [the Oral Torah] already endures, [Scripture] calls it "light." A parable: to what is this comparable? To a hidden chamber at the far end of the house — even though it is day and there is great light in the world, a man cannot see in that chamber unless he brings a lamp into it. So too the Oral Torah: even though it is light, it needs the Written Torah to resolve its difficulties and to explain its secrets.

150. Rabbi Rechumai said: What is that which is written (Proverbs 6:23), "and the way of life is the reproofs of instruction"? This teaches that whoever is accustomed to [engage with] the Work of Creation and the Work of the Chariot cannot but stumble, as it is said (Isaiah 3:6), "and this stumbling-block is under your hand" — matters that a man cannot stand upon unless he has stumbled in them. And the Torah says "reproofs of instruction," but in truth he merits the way of life. Therefore, one who wishes to merit the way of life should endure the reproofs of instruction.

151. Another interpretation: "life" — this is the Torah, as it is said (Deuteronomy 30:19), "and you shall choose life," and it is written (ibid. 20), "for it is your life and the length of your days." And one who wishes to merit it should despise the pleasure of the body and bear the yoke of the commandments; and if sufferings come upon him, let him accept them with love. And let him not say, "since I fulfill the will of my Maker and study Torah every day, why do the sufferings come upon me?" — rather, let him accept them with love, and then he shall merit the complete way of life. For who knows the mind of the Holy One, blessed be He? And one is obligated to say of everything (Psalms 119:137), "You are righteous, O Lord, and Your judgments are upright" — and all that the Heaven does is for good.

152. You said "His throne," and yet we said this is the crown of the Holy One, blessed be He, as we say, "with three crowns Israel were crowned: the crown of priesthood, the crown of kingship, and the crown of Torah above them." A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had a fine and fragrant vessel and loved it greatly. Sometimes he placed it on his head — and that is the tefillin of the head; sometimes he took it upon his arm in the knot of the tefillin of the arm; sometimes he lent it to his son to dwell with him; sometimes it is called his throne, for he bears it on his arm like an amulet, like a kind of throne.

153. And what is the seventh? It is the Aravot of heaven. And why is it called "heaven" (shamayim)? Because it is round like a head, and it teaches [that] water (mayim) is at its right and fire (esh) is at its left, and it is in the middle; and it is that which bears (sa) water (mayim) from fire and from water, and brings peace between them. The fire comes and finds at its side the measure of fire, and the water comes and finds at its side the measure of water — and that is "He makes peace in His heights" (Job 25:2).

154. The seventh is nothing but the sixth. Rather, this teaches that here is the holy Palace, and it bears all of them and is reckoned as two, and so it is the seventh. And what is it? The Thought that has no end and no limit; and so this place has no end and no limit.

155. The seventh is the east of the world, and from there comes the seed of Israel; for the spinal cord is drawn from the brain of man and comes to the member, and from there is the seed, as it is written (Isaiah 43:5), "from the east I will bring your seed" — when Israel are good, from this place "I will bring your seed" and a new seed shall be renewed for you; and when Israel are wicked, [it is] from the seed that has already come into the world, as it is said (Ecclesiastes 1:4), "a generation goes and a generation comes" — teaching that it has already come.

156. And what is that which is written (Isaiah 43:5), "and from the west I will gather you"? From that measure which always inclines to the west. Why is it called "west" (ma'arav)? Because there all the seed is mingled (mit'arev). A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king's son who had a fine and modest bride in his chamber, and he would take wealth from his father's house and bring it to her continually, and she would take it all and store it away continually and mingle it all. At the end of days he wished to see what she had gathered and amassed — and that is what is written, "and from the west I will gather you." And what is it? His father's house, as it is written, "from the east I will bring your seed" — teaching that from the east He brings and sows in the west, and afterward gathers what He sows.

157. What is the eighth? The Holy One, blessed be He, has a single Righteous One (Tzaddik) in His world, and He cherishes him because he sustains the whole world entire, and he is its foundation; and he nourishes it, and causes it to sprout and grow and guards it — beloved and cherished above, beloved and cherished below, awesome and mighty above, awesome and mighty below, established and accepted above, established and accepted below. And he is the foundation of all the souls. And you said "the eighth," and you said "the foundation of all the souls" — yet it is written (Exodus 31:17), "and on the seventh day He rested and was refreshed (vayinafash)"! Is it not the seventh? [Yes,] because it decides between them, for the six are three below and three above, and it decides between them.

158. And why is it called the seventh — was it on the seventh? No. Rather, because the Holy One, blessed be He, rested on the Sabbath, in that measure, as it is written (ibid.), "for in six days the Lord made the heavens and the earth, and on the seventh day He rested and was refreshed." This teaches that each and every day has an utterance that is master over it — not because it was created on it, but because it performs on it the action handed to it. They all performed their action and established their works; therefore the seventh day came and performed its action, and they all rejoiced — even the Holy One, blessed be He. And not only that, but their soul was enlarged, as it is written, "and on the seventh day He rested and was refreshed."

159. And what is this rest, in which there is no labor, and which is a repose, as it is written "he rested"? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had seven gardens, and in the middle garden [was] a spring flowing from a source of living [water], three to its right and three to its left; and as soon as it performs this action or is filled, they all rejoice, for they say, "for our need it is filled." And it waters them and grows them, and they wait and rest, and it waters the seven. But is it not written (Isaiah 43:5), "from the east I will bring your seed" — and this is one of them, and He waters it? Rather, say: He waters the Heart, and the Heart afterward waters them all.

160. Rabbi Berechiah sat and expounded: What is this that we say every day, "the World to Come," and we do not know what we are saying? "The World to Come" we render "the world that is coming (alma de'atei)." What is "the world that is coming"? This teaches that before the world was created, it arose in Thought to create a great light to give light; and a great light was created that no creature could master. The Holy One, blessed be He, foresaw that they could not bear it; He took a seventh and set it in its place for them, and the rest He stored away for the righteous in the time to come. And He said: if they merit this seventh and keep it, I will give them this in the last world. And that is the World to Come, which had already come from before the six days of Creation. That is what is written (Psalms 31:20), "how great is Your goodness which You have hidden for those who fear You," etc.

161. What is that which is written (Exodus 15:27), "And they came to Elim, and there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm-trees, and they camped there by the water"? Now, what praise was there in seventy palm-trees? In one of the smallest places there are a thousand! Rather, they merited [something] like them, and there is a lesson in the palm-trees, as it is written, "And they came to Marah, and could not drink water from Marah, for it was bitter (marim) they were." This teaches that the north wind was mixed in, as it is said (ibid. 25), "and he cried out to the Lord, and the Lord showed him a tree, and he cast it into the water, and the water was sweetened." At once the Holy One, blessed be He, put His hand upon the Satan and diminished him; and had it not been so, Israel could not have stood before him, as it is written (ibid.), "there He set for him a statute and an ordinance, and there He tested him." This teaches that at that hour the Satan clung to them in order to destroy them from the world, as it is written (ibid. 24), "and the people murmured against Moses, saying, what shall we drink?" And further, he accused Moses, until he cried out to the Lord and was answered. And what is "and the Lord showed him a tree"? This teaches that the tree of life was around the water, and the Satan came and took it from there in order to accuse Israel and to make them sin against their Father in heaven. And he would say to them: now you shall enter the wilderness, and while these waters, though bitter, are yet good, for you may make do with them — but in the wilderness you will not find even [enough] to wash your hands and your face, and you shall die of hunger and thirst, naked and in want of all. They came to Moses and said to him thus and so; he rebuffed them. He [the Satan] saw that he could not [prevail over] them, and grew strong against Israel and against Moses; and the people came and murmured against Moses: here we lack water, and what shall we drink in the wilderness? The Satan came and falsified their words to Moses, in order to make them sin. And at once, when Moses saw the Satan, "he cried out to the Lord, and the Lord showed him a tree" — it was that tree of life which the Satan had removed — "and he cast it into the water, and the water was sweetened." There the Holy One, blessed be He, set for the Satan a statute and an ordinance, and there He tested Israel; and the Holy One, blessed be He, came to warn Israel and said, "if you will surely heed the voice of the Lord your God," etc.

162. A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had a beautiful daughter, and others desired her. The king knew of the matter, and could not contend with those who would lead his daughter to evil ways. He came to his house and warned her, saying, "My daughter, do not heed the words of these enemies, and they will not be able [to harm] you; and do not go out the door of the house, and do your work in your house, and do not sit idle even one hour, and they will not be able to see you or to harm you. For there is in them one trait: they keep far from every good way and choose every evil way; and when they see a man directing himself upon a good way and walking in it, they hate him." And what is it? The Satan. This teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, has a measure whose name is Evil (Ra'ah), and it is to the north of the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is written (Jeremiah 1:14), "from the north the evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the earth" — that is to say, all evil that comes upon all the inhabitants of the earth comes from the north.

163. And what is this one measure? It is the form of a hand, and it has many messengers, and the name of all of them is "Evil, evil" — but there is among them small and great, and they render the world guilty; for chaos (tohu) is to the north side, and there is no chaos but evil that confounds the world until it makes them sin. And every evil inclination (yetzer ha-ra) that is in man comes from there. And why was it placed on the left? Because it has no permission in the whole world except in the north, and it is accustomed only to the north, and it wishes to be only in the north; for were it in the south, until it learned the way of the south and how it could mislead, it would tarry those days that it learns, and would not cause sin. Therefore it is always in the north, on the left. And that is what is written (Genesis 8:21), "for the inclination of the heart of man is evil from his youth" — evil is it from his youth, and it inclines only to the left, for it is already accustomed to it. Therefore the Holy One, blessed be He, said to Israel (Exodus 15:26), "if you will surely heed the voice of the Lord your God, and do what is upright in His eyes, and give ear to His commandments" — and not to the commandments of the evil inclination — "and keep all His statutes" — and not the statutes of the evil inclination — "I am the Lord your healer."

164. And what does the evil inclination gain? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who appointed overseers over the provinces of his kingdom, and over his labor, and over his merchandise, and over each and every one an appointee: one appointed over the storehouses of good food, and one appointed over the storehouse of stones. All the world came to buy from the storehouses of good food. This one, appointed over the storehouse of stones, came and saw what they were buying, and was jealous. What did he do? He sent his messengers to demolish the weak houses — for the strong they could not [demolish]. He said: whether this way or that, since they strive to demolish one house of the strong, let them demolish twenty of the weak, and all will come and build with stones from me, and I will not be less than my fellow. That is what is written (Jeremiah 1:14), "from the north the evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the earth," and afterward (ibid. 15), "for behold, I am calling to all the families of the kingdoms of the north, says the Lord, and they shall come and each set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem," etc. And the evil shall be their occupation, and there the evil inclination strives continually. And what is implied? That "Satan" is a term of turning aside (matteh), for it turns the whole world to the scale of guilt; and it is written (Genesis 38:16), "and he turned aside to her by the way," which is rendered "and he turned aside to her," and it is written (Proverbs 4:15), "turn aside from it and pass on."

165. "Seventy palm-trees" (Exodus 15:27) — what is the reason? Rather, they accepted upon themselves the commandments, as it is written (ibid. 26), "if you will surely heed the voice of the Lord your God"; at once "and they came to Elim" (ibid. 27). What is "Elim"? "To me, what (eli mah)." "And there were twelve springs of water": the Holy One, blessed be He, first gave them springs of water, and afterward turned them back into stones, as it is written (Joshua 4:9), "and twelve stones." What is the reason? Because at first the Torah was in the world, likened to water; afterward it was fixed in a fixed place — which the water does not do, for today it is here and tomorrow it is elsewhere.

166. What is "seventy palm-trees" (Exodus 15:27)? This teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, has seventy statures (komot), and they draw from twelve simple ones. Just as this water is simple, so too they are simple. And from where [do we know] that a palm-tree is a stature? As it is written (Song of Songs 7:8), "this your stature is likened to a palm-tree." And not only that, but there are among palm-trees seventy kinds, as it is written "and seventy palm-trees," and they were not alike one to another, nor was the action of this one like that, nor the taste of this like the taste of that.

167. And you said "seventy palm-trees, seventy statures" — yet you said seventy-two! Rather, they are seventy-one, and Israel is seventy-two, but they are not counted [together]. And you said seventy! Rather, the one is the prince of Satan. A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had sons; he bought servants for them and said to them, "Behold, [they are] for you, equally, for all of you." One of them said, "I do not wish [to be] with you, for I have the ability to plunder from all of everything." The king said: since it is so, you shall have no portion among them. And he did according to all his ability; he went out and lay in wait for them on the way, and showed them much gold and precious stones and troops, and said to them, "turn aside to me." What did the king do? He arrayed his troops and all his sons' troops, and showed them to the servants, and said to them: let this deceiver not mislead you by saying "his troops are more numerous than mine." Behold, the troops of that deceitful son think to plunder you, and you shall not heed him; for at first he will smooth his words so that you be caught in his net, and afterward he will mock. But you are my servants, and I will do you every good if you do not heed him and do not turn aside after him — for he is prince over chaos (tohu), as it is said (1 Samuel 12:21), "and do not turn aside, for [it is] after chaos which does not profit and does not save, for chaos they are." But they can harm; and behold, I counsel you (Exodus 15:26), "if you will surely heed the voice of the Lord your God, and do what is upright in His eyes, and give ear to His commandments, and keep all His statutes — when you keep all His statutes — every disease which I set in Egypt I will not set upon you." Why did He say all this? Rather, to close for him [Israel] all the doors, so that it [the evil inclination] not find you at one time soft and at one time hard: "when you keep all His statutes, every disease which I set in Egypt" — by its hand — "I will not set upon you." What is "for I am the Lord your healer"? And when did He smite? Rather, when it comes and smites, "I am the Lord your healer."

168. And what is that which you said, "the eighth"? Because with it the eight begin and with it the eight are completed in the count; yet in action it is the seventh. And what are they that begin? [The circumcision at] eight days of the covenant of circumcision — and though they are eight, they are but seven. And what is the reason the Holy One, blessed be He, said "eight"? Because they are the eight extremities that are in man. And what are they? The right hand and the left, the right foot and the left, the head and the body, and the covenant (berit) that decides — and his wife, who is his mate, as it is written (Genesis 2:24), "and he shall cleave to his wife, and they shall be one flesh"; behold, eight. And these are the eight days of circumcision corresponding to them; and these eight are but seven, for the body and the covenant are one — behold, [with the wife,] eight.

169. What is the ninth? He said to him: the ninth and the tenth are together, one corresponding to the other, and the one is higher than its fellow by five hundred years; and they are like two wheels (ofanim), the one inclining to the north side and the one inclining to the west side, and they go down to the lowest earth. And what is "the lowest"? The last of the seven lands below. And the end of the Shekhinah of the Holy One, blessed be He, is beneath His feet, as it is said (Isaiah 66:1), "the heavens are My throne and the earth is My footstool"; and there is the victory (netzach) of the world below, as it is written (ibid. 34:10), "forever and ever (le-netzach netzachim)."

170. What is "forever and ever (le-netzach netzachim)"? Rather, "netzach" is one — and what is it? This is the one inclining to the west. And its second — what is it? This is the one inclining to the north side. And the third — this is the one below; a third. And you said the two wheels of the Chariot! Rather, say: the end of the Shekhinah is likewise a "netzach." And that is "le-netzach netzachim": "netzach" — one; "netzachim" — two; behold, three.

171. His disciples said to him: from above to below we know, [but] from below to above we do not know. He said to them: is it not one, from below to above and from above to below? They said: our master, the one who ascends is not like the one who descends, for the one who descends descends swiftly, but the one who ascends is not so. He said to them: go out and see. He sat and expounded to them: the Shekhinah is below just as the Shekhinah is above. And what is this Shekhinah? Say: it is the light emanated from the first light, which is Wisdom; it too surrounds everything, as it is said (Isaiah 6:3), "the whole earth is full of His glory." What is its work here? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had seven sons, and he set for each and every one his place. He said to them, "sit, this one upon that one." The lowest one said, "I will not sit below and be far from you." He said to them: behold, I surround and see you all day — and that is "the whole earth is full of His glory." And why is He among them? In order to establish them and sustain them.

172. And who are the sons? I have already told you that the Holy One, blessed be He, has seven holy forms, and they all correspond to [forms] in man, as it is said (Genesis 9:6), "for in the image of God He made man," (ibid. 1:27) "in the image of God He created him, male and female He created them." And these are: the right thigh and the left, the right hand and the left, the body, the covenant, and the head — behold, six. And you said seven? The seventh is with his wife, as it is written (ibid. 2:22, cf.), "and they shall be one flesh." But she was taken from his ribs (side), as it is written (ibid. 21), "and He took one of his ribs (sides)"! He said: yes, from his side. And does He have a side? Yes, as you say (Exodus 26:20), "and for the side (tzela) of the Tabernacle," which we render "and for the side of the Tabernacle." And what side has it? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who resolved in his heart to plant in his garden nine male trees, and they were all palms. He said: when they are all of one kind, they cannot endure. What did he do? He planted a citron (etrog) among them — and it is one of those nine that he had resolved should be male. And what is the citron? Female. And that is what is written (Leviticus 23:40), "the fruit of a goodly tree (hadar), fronds of palms." What is "the fruit of a goodly tree"? As we render it, "the fruit of the tree — citrons and palm-fronds."

173. What is "hadar" (goodly/splendor)? It is the splendor of all; and it is the "hadar" of the Song of Songs, of which it is said (Song of Songs 6:10), "who is this that looks forth like the dawn, fair as the moon, clear as the sun, awesome as the bannered hosts" — and that is on account of the female. And for her sake the female was taken from Adam, for it is impossible for the lower world to endure without a female. And why is she called "female" (nekevah)? Because her orifices (nekavim) are wide, and she has orifices in excess of the man. And what are they? The orifices of the breasts, and the womb, and the receptacle.

174. And what is that which you said, the Song of Songs, which is the "hadar"? Yes, it is the splendor of all the holy books, for Rabbi Yochanan said: all the books are holy, and all the Torah is holy, and the Song of Songs is the holy of holies. And what is the holy of holies? Rather, a holy that is for the holy ones. And what are the holy ones? Those that correspond to the six extremities in man; and it is a holy — a holy for all of them.

175. And what is the "holy"? This is the citron (etrog), which is the splendor of all. And why is it called "hadar"? Do not read "hadar" but "ha-dar" (that which dwells) — this is the citron, which is separate from the bound lulav, and the commandment of the lulav is fulfilled only with it; and it too is bound with all, for with each one it is, and with all of them together it is.

176. The lulav — corresponding to what? Corresponding to the spinal cord. And so it says (Leviticus 23:40), "and a branch of a leafy tree (avot)" — its branches must cover most of it, and if its branches do not cover most of it, it is nothing. Why? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a man who has his arms, with which he shields his head — behold, his two arms and his head are three. And that is "branch (anaf)" for the left, "leafy (avot)" for the right; the tree is found in the middle. And why is "tree" said of it? Because it is the root of the tree.

177. And what is "and willows of the brook (arvei nachal)" (ibid.)? These correspond to the thighs of man, which are two. And what is the meaning of the term "willows of the brook (arvei nachal)"? Because the greater of the two is to the west, and from there it draws its power; and the one of the north is smaller than it by a journey of five hundred years, and it is in the north-west direction, and in it he acts, and it is called by its name — and the two of them are "arvim" (mingled/pledged).

178. Another interpretation: "willows of the brook" — for at times they mingle (me'arvim) their actions one with another. And what is "and willows of the brook (arvei nachal)"? Say: on account of the place in which they are fixed, whose name is "brook (nachal)," as it is written (Ecclesiastes 1:7), "all the brooks go to the sea, and the sea is not full." And what is this sea? Say: this is the citron. And from where [do we know] that each and every one of these seven is called a "brook"? As it is written (Numbers 21:19), "and from Mattanah, Nachaliel" — do not read "Nachaliel" but "brooks of God (nachalei El)." And all six go by one way to the sea. And what is that one way that decides between them? As it is written (Habakkuk 3:5), "before Him goes pestilence, and a fiery bolt goes forth at His feet." And they all go to that channel, and from that channel to the sea. And that is what is written, "and from Mattanah, Nachaliel" — from the place where [the point] was given, which is the brain, to Nachaliel; and from Nachaliel, Bamot; and what is Bamot? As it is rendered "Ramata." What is Ramata? That "segulah" (segol) that is after the "zarka." "And from Bamot, the valley that is in the field of Moab, the top of Pisgah, and it looks out over the face of the wasteland" — "and from Bamot, the valley that is in the field of Moab," which is prepared; and what is that which is in the field of Moab? Do not read "Moab" but "from the father (me-av)" — that father of whom it is said (Genesis 26:5), "because Abraham heeded My voice and kept My charge, My commandments, My statutes," etc. And what is that field? That which is at the top of Pisgah and also looks out over the face of the wasteland, which is rendered "the heavens." And of that channel it is said (Song of Songs 4:15), "a fountain of gardens, a well of living waters, and flowing streams from Lebanon." And what is Lebanon? Say: this is Wisdom. And what is "willows of the brook"? Say: that which gave an inheritance (nachalah) to Israel — and that is the two wheels of the Chariot.

179. It was taught: there are ten spheres (galgalim) and ten utterances, and each and every sphere has an utterance — not that [the utterance] revolves within it, but that it makes it revolve. This world is like a mustard-seed in a ring. Why? Because of the spirit that blows in it, and by this it stands; and were the spirit to cease for one hour or one moment, the world would be destroyed.

180. This world has three spheres — and how? This world inclines to the north side and to the south — how? North-west, south. North-west is the first sphere that revolves over us. North-west — does it enter your mind? Rather, say: its power is north — and that is the left thigh. And upon it is a second sphere, and it is entirely west. West — does it enter your mind? Rather, say: its power is west — these are the "victories (netzachim)" of the world. And upon it is a third sphere, and its power is south-west. And what is the first power that you called the second? Say: the right thigh. And this power of south-west is the foundation of the world, of which it is said (Proverbs 10:25), "and the righteous one is the foundation of the world." And the second power stands behind the Chariot, and its first power is before it, and "the righteous one, the foundation of the world" is in the middle. It goes forth from the south of the world, and it is prince over these two; also in its hand is the soul of all living, for it is the Life of the worlds; and every term of creation is done through it. And of it is written (Exodus 31:17), "He rested and was refreshed"; and it is the measure of the Sabbath day, and of it is said (ibid. 20:8), "Remember the Sabbath day to sanctify it." But is it not also written (Deuteronomy 5:12), "Keep"? That [refers] to the seventh measure; and of the seventh, Scripture said (Leviticus 19:30), "My Sabbaths you shall keep, and My sanctuary you shall revere." And what is the seventh measure? This is the measure of the goodness of the Holy One, blessed be He.

181. And what is the reason [Scripture] said (ibid.), "My Sabbaths you shall keep," and did not say "My Sabbath"? A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had a beautiful bride, and every week he appointed one day for her to be with him; and the king has fine sons whom he loves. He said to them: since it is so, rejoice, you too, on the day of my rejoicing, for it is for your sake that I strive; and you too, honor me.

182. And what is the reason [it says] "Remember" and "Keep"? "Remember" for the male, and "Keep" for the bride. And what is the reason "and My sanctuary you shall revere"? Guard yourselves from [impure] thought, for My sanctuary is holy. Why? For "I am the Lord who sanctifies you" in all.

183. And what is the reason we say, of all that He created, etc., "Life of the worlds," and we do not say "of all that You created"? Rather, we bless the Holy One, blessed be He, who pours out His Wisdom to this "Life of the worlds," and He gives all.

184. And what is the reason we say, "who sanctified us with His commandments and commanded us," and we do not say, "who sanctified You and commanded You"? This teaches that in the Life of the worlds are included all the commandments entire, and in His mercy upon us He gave them to us, in order to sanctify us by them, that perhaps we may merit. And why? For at the hour that we merit in this world [to reach] the World to Come, which is great — and in His hand is the treasury of all the souls — and at the hour that Israel are good, the souls merit to go forth and come into this world; and if they are not good, they do not go forth. And that is what we say: "the son of David does not come until all the souls in the body are exhausted." And what is "all the souls in the body"? Say: all the souls in the body of man; and [when these are exhausted] the new ones will merit to go forth — and then the son of David comes, and merits to be born, for his soul goes forth new among the rest. A parable: to what is this comparable? To a king who had an army, and he sent them bread and food in abundance, and they were lazy and did not eat it, nor did they guard it, and it became moldy and the bread was spoiled. He came to inquire and to visit, [to see] whether they had what to eat, and whether they had eaten what he had sent them; he found them [with] moldy bread, and found them ashamed to ask for other bread — as if to say, "this we did not guard, and shall we ask for other?" The king too was angry, and took that moldy bread and commanded that it be dried and repaired as much as possible, and swore to these men: other bread I will not give them until they eat all this moldy bread. He sent to them anew. What did they do? They said, let us divide it; they divided it, and each one of them took his portion. The diligent one placed his portion in the air and guarded it and ate well; and the other took it and ate of it greedily — he ate what he ate, and the rest he left and did not guard it, for he despaired of it, and it was spoiled further and grew moldy, and he could not eat it at all, and he remained hungry until he died. The iniquity was reckoned against his body: why did you kill yourself? Is it not enough that you spoiled the bread for yourselves at first, and I returned it to you repaired, and you spoiled it — but you spoiled your own portion and were negligent in guarding it; and not only that, but you killed yourself! And he answers, "My lord, what should I have done?" And [the king] answers him: you should have guarded it; and if you say you could not, you should have observed your fellow and your neighbors who shared the bread with you, and seen their deeds and their guarding, and striven to guard as they did. And further they inquire of him: why did you kill yourself? Is it not enough that you spoiled the bread, but you added and destroyed the matter of your body and shortened your days, or caused [it to be shortened]? And perhaps a worthy son would have gone forth from you who would have saved you, and your spoiling, and others, and their spoiling. Therefore from every side sufferings multiply upon you. He was terrified and answered, "and what could I have done, since I had no bread, and from what could I have lived?" They say to him: had you toiled and labored in the Torah, you would not have answered such folly and insolence; for from the answer of your words it is clear that you did not toil or labor in Torah. Is it not written in the Torah (Deuteronomy 8:3), "for not by bread alone does man live, but by all that goes forth from the mouth of the Lord does man live"? You should have inquired and searched and asked what is the thing by which man lives. And what is "that which goes forth from the mouth of the Lord"? Say: he lives by the Torah, which is "that which goes forth from the mouth of the Lord." From here they said: "an ignoramus (am ha'aretz) is not pious" — for if he does not do kindness to himself, how can he be called pious?

185. And how does one do kindness with his Maker? Through the study of Torah; for whoever studies Torah does kindness with his Maker, as it is written (Deuteronomy 33:26), "who rides the heavens in your help" — that is to say, when you study Torah for its own sake, then you help Me, and I ride the heavens; and then "and in His majesty the skies" (ibid.). And what are "the skies (shechakim)"? Say: in the innermost chambers, as it is rendered "and His word is in the heaven of heavens." Therefore, "not by bread alone does man live, but by all that goes forth from the mouth of the Lord" — which is the Torah that went forth from the mouth of the Lord — "by it does man live." And "a fool answers with insolence"; forsake insolence and do not answer as you answered. Therefore he was punished; and what is his punishment — I have already explained it.

186. What is that which is written (Job 15:2), "shall a wise man answer with windy knowledge (da'at ruach)"? What is "windy knowledge"? Rather, knowledge that is close to the spirit (ruach), as it is written (Isaiah 11:2), "and the spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding" — Wisdom, and after it Understanding; and in Understanding [are] counsel and might and knowledge and the fear of the Lord. And you told us that counsel is the doing of kindness, and Understanding is the measure of judgment, [and] knowledge is Truth, and so with knowledge one discerns the truthful man; [and] the fear of the Lord is the treasury of the Torah. And that is what I say, only that this is above; for Rabbi Akiva said: all that the Holy One, blessed be He, created, He created corresponding [to something], as it is said (Ecclesiastes 7:14), "also this corresponding to that God made." And what is the treasury of the Torah? As it is written (Isaiah 33:6), "the fear of the Lord is His treasure." Therefore a man must first be God-fearing, and afterward study Torah. A parable: to a man who comes to buy honeyed dates, and did not bring a vessel to carry them. He said, "I will bring them in my bosom." He brought them in his bosom; their burden was heavy upon him; he feared lest they tear and soil the garments, and he cast them on the way. He was punished twice: once for their loss — [others'] food — and once for the loss of his money.

187. And this fear of the Lord that is above — it is in the palm of the Holy One, blessed be He, and it too is His might; and that palm is called the "palm of merit" (kaf zechut), because it inclines the world to the scale of merit. And that is what is written (Isaiah 11:3), "and he shall smell with the fear of the Lord, and not by the sight of his eyes shall he judge, nor by the hearing of his ears shall he reprove" — rather, he inclines the whole world to the scale of merit; and from there counsel goes forth, and from there health goes forth to the world; and "from there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel" (Genesis 49:24). And it is the place that is called "there," as it is said (Habakkuk 3:4), "and there is the hiding of His might."

188. And since this matter came, sharpen it. What is the sharpening? Tell us: what is "and rays (karnayim) from His hand to Him" (Habakkuk 3:4)? And what is it that he says "rays," and afterward says "His hand (yado)" — it should be "His hands (yadav)"? It is no difficulty, for that is what is written (Exodus 32:19), "and Moses' anger burned, and he cast from his hands (mi-yadav) the tablets," etc. — [yet] "from his hand (mi-yado)" is [also] written; and that is what is written (ibid. 17:12), "and his hands (yadav) were faith (emunah) until the sun set" — and it does not say "faiths (emunot)." They said to him: our master, we ask you to resolve, and you cover its matter! You taught us, our master, "one answers regarding the first [question] first and regarding the last last." And what is it you ask? "Rays from His hand to Him" — by the [Temple] service, I have already explained it to you now amid my words. They were ashamed. Since he saw that they were ashamed, he began and said to them: was it not water, and from it went forth fire? They said to him, [these are] words of vanity — if so, the water includes the fire! Master, and what are the "rays"? He said to them: five rays, [namely] the five fingers of the right hand of man.

189. "But, our master, you are the one who told us in the name of Rabbi Yochanan that they are but the two arms of the world!" He said to them: yes; but here "rays (karnayim)" serves for two owners (konim) that are below it. And what are they? He said to them: in anger, [it is] your head; and what is above? He said to them: the fear of the Lord.

190. And what is the fear of the Lord? The first light, of which Rabbi [Yochanan] said: What is that which is written (Genesis 1:3), "and God said, let there be light, and there was light," and it does not say "and it was so"? Rather, this teaches that that light was very great, and no creature could gaze upon it; [so] the Holy One, blessed be He, stored it for the righteous in the time to come. And it is the measure of all the merchandise (sechorah) in the world, and it is the power of the precious stone that is called "socheret and dar." And what is the measure of "dar"? Rather, this teaches that the Holy One, blessed be He, took one part in a thousand of its radiance and built from it a precious stone, fair and adorned, and included in it all the commandments. And Abraham came and sought a power to be given to him, and this precious stone was given to him, and he did not want it — he merited and took his measure, Kindness (chesed), as it is written (Micah 7:20), "kindness to Abraham." Isaac came and sought a power, and it was given to him, and he did not want it — he merited and took the measure of Might (gevurah), which is Fear (pachad), as it is written (Genesis 31:53), "and Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac." Jacob came and wanted it, and it was not given to him; they said to him: since Abraham is above and Isaac below him, you shall be in the middle and take all three. And what is the middle? It is Peace (shalom). But is it not written (Micah, ibid.), "You shall grant truth to Jacob"? Truth and Peace are one, as you say (Esther 9:30), "words of peace and truth," (2 Kings 20:19) "for there shall be peace and truth in my days." And that is what is written (Isaiah 58:14), "and I will feed you the inheritance of Jacob your father" — that is a complete inheritance, for it has Kindness and Fear and Truth and Peace. And therefore it is said (Psalms 118:22), "the stone which the builders rejected has become the head cornerstone" — the stone which Abraham and Isaac, who built the world, rejected, has now become the head cornerstone.

191. And why did they reject it? Is it not said (Genesis 26:5), "because Abraham heeded My voice and kept My charge (mishmarti), My commandments, My statutes, and My teachings"? What is "My charge"? Thus said the measure of Kindness: all the days that Abraham was in the world, I did not need to do my work, for Abraham stood there in my place — "and he kept My charge"; for this is my work, that I acquit the world, and even if they are guilty, I acquit them; and further, I return them and put into their heart to do the will of their Father in heaven. And all this Abraham did, as it is written (ibid. 21:33), "and he planted a tamarisk (eshel) in Beersheba, and called there upon the name of the Lord, God of the world" — he arranged his bread and his water for all who came into the world, and would acquit them and speak to their heart: "whom do you serve? Serve the Lord, God of heaven and earth"; and he would expound to them until they repented. And from where [do we know] that even the guilty he would acquit? As it is said (ibid. 18:17), "shall I hide from Abraham what I am doing, seeing that Abraham shall surely become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed through him?" Rather, I will acquit him, for I know that he will seek mercy upon them and acquit [them]. Now, is it possible to say that the Holy One, blessed be He, did not know that they [Sodom] could not be saved? Rather, He said it for his [Abraham's] merit. From here they said: one who comes to purify himself is helped; one who comes to defile himself is given an opening. What is "given an opening"? Those [doors] that are always open.

192. "My commandments, My statutes, and My teachings (torotai)" (ibid. 26:5). He said: since I did not desire it [the precious stone], I will keep its commandments. And what is "My teachings"? Rather, even the rulings and the dialectics that are studied above — he knew them and fulfilled them.

193. And what is "from there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel" (Genesis 49:24)? That from there the Stone of Israel is nourished. And what is "from there"? Say: the supernal Righteous One (Tzaddik). And what is he? He is the great, hidden light; and that is "socheret." And the stone that dwells below it is called "dar." And what are the "rays," as it is said (Habakkuk 3:4), "rays from His hand to Him"? These are the five fingers of the right hand.

194. Rabbi Rechumai said: thus I have received — when Moses sought to know the knowledge of the honored and awesome Name, blessed be He, and said (Exodus 33:18), "show me, please, Your glory," he sought to know why the righteous one has good and the righteous one has evil, the wicked one has good and the wicked one has evil, and they did not make it known to him. "They did not make it known to him" — does it enter your mind? Rather, they did not make known to him what he sought. And can it enter your mind that Moses did not know this secret? Rather, thus said Moses: I know the ways of the powers, but I do not know how the Thought spreads within it; I know that in the Thought is the Truth, but I do not know its portions — and he sought to know, and they did not make it known to him.

195. Why does the righteous one have good, and the righteous one have evil? Because this righteous one was already wicked in the past, and now he is punished. And is one punished for the days of youth? Did not Rabbi Simon say that one is not punished in the court above except from the age of twenty years and upward? He said to him: I do not speak of [this] life; I speak of that which was already in the past. His companions said to him: how long will you conceal your words? He said to them: go out and see. A parable: to what is this comparable? To a man who planted a vineyard in his garden and hoped it would make grapes, and it made sour grapes. He saw that it did not succeed; he planted it [again], fenced it and breached it, and cleansed the vines of the sour grapes, and planted it a second time. He saw that it did not succeed; he fenced it, planted it after he had cleansed it, and saw that it did not succeed; and he uprooted it and planted it [again]. Until how many [times]? He said to him: to a thousand generations, as it is written (Psalms 105:8), "the word He commanded for a thousand generations." And that is what they said: nine hundred and seventy-four generations were lacking, and the Holy One, blessed be He, arose and planted them in each and every generation.

196. Rabbah said: if the righteous wished, they could create a world. What separates? Your iniquities, as it is written (Isaiah 59:2), "but your iniquities have separated between you and your God" — for had your iniquities not been, there would be no difference between you and Him. For Rabbah created a man and sent him before Rabbi Zeira; he was speaking to him and [the man] did not answer. And were it not for your iniquities, he would have answered. And from what would he have answered? From his soul. And does a man have a soul to be placed in [such a creature]? Yes, as it is written (Genesis 2:7), "and He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life." And a man has the breath of life; and were it not for your iniquities — for the soul is not pure — and that is the difference between you and Him, as it is written (Psalms 8:6), "and You have made him lack a little of God." What is "a little"? That he has iniquities, and the Holy One, blessed be He, has none — and blessed is He and blessed is His name forever and ever. Iniquities He has none, but as for the [evil] inclination — from where does it come? "Comes" — does it enter your mind? Rather, say: it was coming from Him, until David came and slew it. That is what is written (Psalms 109:22), "and my heart is slain within me." Thus said David: since I could not [overcome] it, "evil shall not dwell with You" (Psalms 5:5). And by what did David overcome it? By his study (girsa), for he was not silent night or day, and he would join the Torah above; for whenever a man studies Torah for its own sake, the Torah above joins to the Holy One, blessed be He. And that is what we say: a man should always study Torah, even not for its own sake, for out of [study] not for its own sake, he comes to [study] for its own sake. And what is this Torah of which you speak? It is the bride who is adorned and crowned and comprised of all the commandments; and she is the treasury of the Torah; and she is the betrothed of the Holy One, blessed be He, as it is written (Deuteronomy 33:4), "the Torah that Moses commanded us is the inheritance (morashah) of the congregation of Jacob" — do not read "inheritance (morashah)" but "betrothed (me'orasah)." How so? When Israel engage in the Torah for its own sake, she is the betrothed of the Holy One, blessed be He; and at the time she is the betrothed of the Holy One, blessed be He, she is an inheritance to Israel.

197. Rabbi Amorai sat and expounded: why did Tamar merit that Peretz and Zerach came forth from her? Because her name was Tamar (palm). But Tamar the sister of Amnon — was she made for this? And what is the reason they are called Peretz and Zerach? Peretz is called for the moon, for the moon is at times breached (nifretzet) and is built [again] in the future; and Zerach is called for the sun, for the sun shines (zorachat) always in one manner. But Peretz was the firstborn, and the sun is greater than the moon! It is no difficulty, for it is written (Genesis 38:28), "and one put out a hand," and it is written (ibid. 30), "and afterward his brother came out, on whose hand was the scarlet thread, and his name was called Zerach" — for he should have been the firstborn; but because of the joy of the Holy One, blessed be He, who foresaw that Solomon was destined to come forth from him, who was destined to speak the Song of Songs, He returned him.

198. And what is the reason she was called Tamar and not other names? Because she is female. Female — does it enter your mind? Rather, because she comprises male and female, for all palm-trees comprise male and female. And how? The lulav (frond) is male; and the fruit — on the outside it is male, and on the inside it is female. And how? In the pit of the date, which is cleft like a woman; and corresponding to her is the power of the moon above. And the Holy One, blessed be He, created man male and female, as it is said (Genesis 1:27), "male and female He created them." Is it possible to say so? Is it not written (ibid.), "and God created the man in His image, in the image of God He created him," and afterward (ibid. 2:18), "I will make him a helper corresponding to him," (ibid. 21) "and He took one of his ribs and closed up flesh in its place"? Rather, say: [the term] "formation" (yetzirah) is written of them, and "making" (asiyah) is written, and "creation" (beri'ah) is written, at the time of the making of the soul. "Making" — male and female He created them. "Formation" — at the time He mounted the soul upon the body and gathered the spirit. And from where [do we know] that this "formation" (yetzirah) is a term of gathering? As it is written (ibid. 19), "and the Lord God formed (vayitzer) from the ground every beast of the field and every bird of the heavens, and brought [them] to the man to see what he would call it," etc. And that is what is written (ibid. 5:2), "male and female He created them," and it is written (ibid.), "and God blessed them."

199. The soul of the female [is] from the female, and the soul of the male from the male. And that is why the serpent went after Eve; he said, since her soul is from the north side, I will seduce her quickly. And what seduction was in it? Because he came upon her.

200. His disciples asked him: tell us how the matter was. He said to them: Samael the wicked made a bond with all the hosts above against his Master, because the Holy One, blessed be He, said (Genesis 1:28), "and have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens." He said: how can we cause him [Adam] to sin and drive him out from before Him? He descended with all his hosts and sought for himself on earth a companion like himself, and found the serpent, which had the likeness of a camel; and he mounted upon it and went to the woman. He said to her (Genesis 3:1), "even though God said [not to eat] of every tree of the garden" — but I will ask more and add, so that she diminish. She said, "we are forbidden only the fruit of the tree that is in the midst of the garden; God said, you shall not eat of it and shall not touch it, lest you die" — and she added two things: she said "and the fruit of the tree that is in the midst of the garden," though [God] had said only "and of the tree of knowledge"; and she said "you shall not touch it, lest you die." What did Samael the wicked do? He went and touched the tree, and the tree cried out and said, "Wicked one, do not touch me!" as it is said (Psalms 36:12), "let not the foot of pride come upon me, and let not the hand of the wicked drive me away." There the workers of iniquity fell — they were thrust down and could not rise. He went and said to the woman, "Behold, I touched the tree and did not die; you too, touch it and you will not die." The woman went and touched the tree, and saw the Angel of Death coming toward her. She said, "Woe is me, now I shall die, and the Holy One, blessed be He, will make another woman and give her to Adam. Rather, behold, I will cause him to eat with me: if we die, we both die, and if we live, we both live." And she took and ate of the fruits of the tree and gave also to her husband. His eyes were opened and his teeth were set on edge. He said to her, "What is this you have fed me, that has set my teeth on edge? So have the teeth of all the generations been set on edge." He [God] sat in true judgment, as it is said (Psalms 9:5), "He judges [in] righteousness." He called to Adam and said to him, "Why did you flee from before Me?" He said to Him, "I heard Your voice in the garden, and my bones trembled, and I was afraid, for I am naked, and I hid" (Genesis 3:10) — for I am naked of my deeds, for I am naked of Your commandment, for I am naked of my [good] works, as it is said, "for I am naked, and I hid." What was Adam's garment? A skin of nail (or fingernail-like light). When he ate of the fruits of the tree, that skin of nail was stripped from upon him, and he saw himself naked, as it is said (ibid. 11), "who told you that you are naked?" Adam said before the Holy One, blessed be He, "Master of all the worlds, when I was alone, did I sin against You? Rather, the woman whom You brought to me — she seduced me from Your words," as it is said (ibid. 12), "the woman whom You gave to be with me." The Holy One, blessed be He, said to her, "Is it not enough for you that you sinned, but you [also] caused Adam to sin?" She said before Him, "Master of all the worlds, the serpent enticed me to sin before You." He brought the three of them and decreed upon them a judgment of nine curses and death; and He cast down Samael the wicked and his band from their holy place in heaven; and He cut off the legs of the serpent and cursed it more than every beast of the field and every animal, and decreed upon it that it should shed its skin after seven years. And Samael was punished, being made the prince of Esau the wicked. In the future, when the Holy One, blessed be He, uproots the kingdom of Edom — speedily in our days — He will first bring it low, as it is said (Isaiah 24:21), "the Lord shall visit upon the host of the height on high." And the speech, and the death, and the punishment — all this was because she added to the command of the Holy One, blessed be He; and of this it is said, "whoever adds, subtracts." And may the Name enlighten our eyes with the light of His Torah, and place His fear in our hearts, and grant us to meet Him — He who lights up the hearts, and gives sight to the understanding heart, and makes the eyes shine with radiance.
